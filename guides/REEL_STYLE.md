# Reel style (English; the owner's house style for all English scripts and reels; from the owner's sample reels: King Shibi, Lakshmana's sleep, Yudhishthir and the Yaksha)
- 170-250 words, 1-2 min (about 70-100 s at 0.40 s/word; the owner's 3 sample reels are shorter at ~150 words, both are fine). One story, one idea.
- Line 1 = the most shocking or surprising fact (context at once). Or a popular claim snapped back: "<claim>. Well, not really." (contrarian snapback, see .claude/skills/triple-hook).
- Name the source early; give exact location only if the source file has it. Never invent verse numbers.
- Short plain past-tense sentences, one action each, mostly indirect speech; no filler, no decoration, no moral lecture.
- Build step by step (demand -> compliance -> bigger demand; or claim -> source -> origin of the myth).
- Ending in the last 2-3 sentences, one of: twist/reveal, respect restored after a myth is corrected, or one open question that invites comments. Story is complete in every case.
- Each reel is self-contained: re-introduce every name; no "as we saw".
- English is the primary language from EP07: this style also governs the long-form English script (short plain past-tense sentences, one action each, numbers as words, no filler). Word range of a full video: `guides/SCRIPT_STYLE.md`.
- **Every reel answers a question (owner's rule, reels 8-14 on).** The triple hook raises ONE clear question tied to the title; the body answers it explicitly and plainly before the end; the last line states the answer/payoff. Never end on an open editorial question. The comment CTA, if any, lives in the caption, not the script. Each reel file lists `Question:` and `Answer:` under the hook.
- **Reel title = the question the reel answers**, with character names (owner's rule, reels 8-14 on). Examples: "Why Did the Gods Cut Off Vishnu's Head?", "How Did Shiva Escape Bhasmasura?", "Who Was Gajendra the Elephant Before Vishnu Saved Him?". First-frame title = a shortened form of the same question (about 6 words max). The hook's last line raises this question; the last line of the reel answers it.

- **Shock-first hook (owner's rule, from reel 17 on, every story).** The first 2-3 lines must be so shocking or unbelievable that a stranger thinks 'what?!'. Open with the title question, then the most shocking fact of the story (a god tearing out his own eye, a demon's body turning into jewels), then 'why' or 'for whom'. Never open with background, names or setup; no spoilers of the answer.

## Opposite-notion hook (owner's rule, applies to EVERY hook, title, thumbnail text and script from now on)
Say the OPPOSITE of what people believe about a character, a claim that sounds almost impossible, as a deliberate clickbait hook. The viewer must think "that cannot be true", then the story breaks their old belief and ends with "so that is why it happened".
- Pick the quality the character is famous for (Shiva fears nothing, Vishnu is all-powerful, Indra protects, Garuda serves, Narada helps) and state the reverse as the hook or title.
- Examples: "The only demon Shiva ran from" / "The only demon Shiva was afraid of" (Bhasmasura). "The demon who wanted to die" (Bala). "The god who tore out his own eye" (Vishnu). "Did Vishnu cheat a devotee?".
- The hook is true to the story, only framed as the opposite. The body must show the real reason, so the claim is never a lie (for example Shiva ran because of his own boon and his own word).
- Use it for: reel/episode title, first-frame text, thumbnail text (2 short lines, key word in yellow), the first 2-3 spoken lines, captions. Combine with the shock-first rule above: title, then the shocking opposite fact, then no repeated question.
- Short, simple plain English. No spoiler of the answer in the hook.

**File name rule (owner):** whenever a video is sent to the owner, the file name must be the video title (for example "EP07 - Kacha Steals the Secret of the Dead.mp4" or "Reel 5 - How Was Shiva Born.mp4"), so the downloaded file has its title as the name. Copy the video with `studio/titled_copy.sh <video> "<Title>"` and send that copy with SendUserFile. Never send files named like `ep07_scored_1080p.mp4`.

- **Name the character in the hook (owner):** the VERY FIRST SENTENCE (and the title and thumbnail text; the first 2 to 3 spoken lines at most) of every reel and episode, and its title and thumbnail text, must name the main character (or the object, for a story about one), so the viewer knows at once who the story is about. Never open with "a man", "a demon" or "someone" for a named figure.

- **Keep it simple (owner):** the narration must be easy for an Indian listener who knows everyday English: short plain sentences, common words, one idea each, a clear story told in order. No abstract or "wise" lines (for example "being able to do a job and being the one meant to do it are two different things"). If a sentence needs a second listen, rewrite it with simpler words. Prefer "belly button", "hugged", "caught" over fancy words; no words like govern, beneath, propitiate, mannered phrases or idioms.

- **Endings (owner):** end a reel with a question only if it is a real debate about the story that the viewer can answer in a few words (for example "Which one do you think is the strongest?"). Never ask empty questions ("What do you think?", "Would you have saved X?", "Did you know...?"). If there is no good question, end on the last strong fact of the story. Do not claim an epithet or title for a god unless it is a real traditional name (for example Kalantaka for Shiva, the one who ended Yama); mark anything unsure with (check).

- **No invented calls to action (owner):** never write "comment X for the full video", "follow", "like" or "tell us in the comments" in a script unless the owner asks for it. The only thing allowed at the end is a good question about the story itself, or the last strong fact.
