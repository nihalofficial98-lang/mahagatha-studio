=== Did Shiva Create the First Humans? | Did Shiva Create the First Humans? Brahma Could Not Do It Alone ===

Everyone says Brahma, the Creator, made the first humans.
But Brahma could not do it. His own sons had rejected him, and creation was wearing him down.
So who showed him how? The one god he believed could not create.
Vishnu, the Preserver, noticed Brahma's grief. He told him to go to Mahadeva, another name for Shiva. Brahma said the Destroyer could not create. Vishnu said Shiva might not create, but he would surely counsel.
Brahma found Shiva meditating. He sat down and prayed from the depth of his soul. Shiva opened his eyes. He said reproduction could not happen the way Brahma had made his sons. What Brahma needed was Shakti, the power of the feminine.
Then Shiva's body changed. The right side became male. The left side became female, split evenly down the middle. This form was called Ardhanarishwara, the Androgynous One.
Shiva said masculine and feminine were the two halves of the cosmos. Neither could work without the other.
Brahma bowed to the Goddess and asked to create the female. She gave him the generative force. From his own body, Brahma made a man named Manu and a woman named Brahmi. The human race descends from Manu.
The Destroyer had not made the first humans. He had shown the Creator what creation needed, both halves.

=== Why Do People Go to Kashi? | Why Do People Go to Kashi? It Started With Shiva and Brahma's Head ===

Why do people travel to Kashi, also called Varanasi, to be freed of their sins?
The answer begins with Brahma, the Creator, who once had five heads.
Shiva cut one off in rage. Then it would not let go of him.
It began with a curse. The sage Narada had said Brahma would desire what must not be desired. When Brahma created his daughter Brahmi, the curse took hold. She saw his gaze and moved away. Each time she moved, a new head grew so he could keep looking at her.
His sons Marichi and Atri warned him. Brahmi became a cow, a mare, a duck, a doe, a hundred forms in all. She was named Shatarupa.
Shiva appeared in fury. Brahma came to his senses and begged forgiveness. Shiva cut off the fifth head. Brahma's four mouths would recite the Vedas forever, to repent.
Then Shiva stopped. The severed head was stuck to his left hand.
Killing a Brahmin was a sin. Atri said every action brings its result, whatever the reason. Brahma had paid, though a curse drove him. So must Shiva.
Shiva wandered the universe, but nothing freed him. The flesh rotted away until only a skull remained. He was called Kapali.
Then he reached a place on Earth, and the skull fell from his hand. Anyone who came there would be freed of every sin. That place became Kashi, also called Varanasi, and that is why people still go there.

=== Madhu and Kaitabha | How Did Vishnu Defeat Madhu and Kaitabha? ===

Vishnu, the Preserver, fought two brothers for five thousand years and could not win. Then he put down his mace and offered them a gift. But the gift was never for them.
The brothers were Madhu and Kaitabha. They crawled out of Vishnu's earwax when Brahma, the Creator, brought forth the Vedas. They fed on the endless water and grew huge.
They found Brahma on his lotus and told him to bow or fight. He stayed silent, so they shook the stem. The Vedas slipped from his hands, and the brothers hid them.
Brahma begged Vishnu, but Vishnu lay in Yoga Nidra, a trance between waking and sleep. Brahma called on Nidra to release him. Vishnu woke as Hayagriva, the Horse-Headed One.
When one brother tired, the other took over. Vishnu saw strength would never win. So he laid down his mace, praised their power, and offered a boon.
The brothers sneered. Victors did not beg for boons, they said. They gave them. They told him to ask. Vishnu asked, "Grant me that I may kill you both."
They had walked into a trap, but they had given their word. They asked to die only where there was no water. Vishnu grew, towering over the waters like a blue mountain. He placed the brothers on his thighs and beheaded them.
For killing Madhu, Vishnu came to be called Madhusudan. Brahma asked him to keep saving them.
Vishnu said, "Perhaps I did nothing. Perhaps the universe saved itself through me."

=== Narada's Maya Test | Narada Asked Vishnu What Maya Is. Then This Happened ===

Narada, a great sage, asked Vishnu, the Preserver, what maya is. Vishnu did not explain. He asked for water. Narada never brought it, and that was the answer.
Narada had come to Vaikuntha, Vishnu's home, to tell him how his curse had come true on his father, Brahma. Vishnu smiled and said it was all maya. Narada asked what that meant.
Vishnu said he was thirsty. He told Narada to fetch some water, and then he would answer.
Narada walked to the river outside. By the bank he saw a young woman. She was so lovely that he forgot Vishnu, his errand and his thirst.
He married her right there and built a house on the sand by the river.
Years passed. Narada became a father, then a grandfather. The house had become his whole world.
Then one day the sky turned grey. Thunder roared. Rain poured, and the river flooded. The water swept his family away.
Suddenly Narada was back in Vaikuntha, standing before Vishnu. Vishnu asked, "Where is the water? I told you I am thirsty."
Narada stood stunned. His wife, his children, his home. None of it had been real.
Vishnu said that was maya. Attachment to a world that does not last. It makes us chase what slips away like sand.
Then he smiled and warned Narada that there would be much more maya to come.

=== Shiva Born From Anger | How Was Shiva Born From Brahma's Anger? ===

Shiva was born from another god's anger. His first demand was not for power. It was for a name.
Brahma, the Creator, had made four sons from his mind. They were child sages called the Kumaras. He told them to help him fill the world with living beings. They folded their hands and said no.
They said they were grateful, but they did not want to be bound by life and death. They wanted to meditate on the infinite. They asked only that they never grow old. Brahma argued, then gave in. As his sons left, rage rose in him.
A fiery beam burst from his forehead. A being stepped out of it with blazing red eyes. He roared, "What is my name?"
Brahma sat in awe. The being roared again. "Tell me my name." Brahma remembered that the root word for both red and roaring is Rud. He said, "Rudra. I name you Rudra."
This was Shiva, also called Mahadeva. Brahma created, Vishnu preserved, and Shiva was the Destroyer.
Brahma asked him to help with creation. Shiva agreed and began making beings. But he made copies of himself, and each was immortal. Eleven Rudras stood before Brahma.
Brahma said creation needed mortals, who die and are born again. Shiva said he could only make immortals. If that spoiled the plan, he would rather meditate. Then he left.
The god born from Brahma's anger could only make what never dies.

=== Why Say Swaha? | Why Does Every Offering to Agni End With Swaha? ===

Every offering to the fire god Agni ends with the word Swaha. The gods once starved while humans fed the fire regularly. The fire god himself said it was his fault.
One morning, the gods came to Brahma, the Creator, in Brahmaloka. They were thin and weak. They had nothing to eat.
Brahma explained how they were meant to eat. When humans held a yagna, a fire ritual, they poured offerings into the fire. Agni, the mouth of the gods, carried them to heaven. In return, the gods gave humans blessings.
Indra, a leader of the gods, said humans held yagnas all the time. But nothing reached heaven.
Another god, Kamadeva, blamed Agni. Agni stepped forward with folded hands. He said he was to blame. He could burn the offerings, but he could not carry them to the gods. He did not have that skill.
Brahma had no answer. Vishnu, the Preserver, told him to pray to Prakriti, Mother Nature.
Prakriti created a young goddess to carry offerings from Earth to heaven. Brahma asked her name.
"Swaha," she said.
Brahma declared that every offering to Agni must end with her name. Without it, the offering would not reach the gods. A chant without Swaha would be like a tree without fruit, a snake without venom, a man without knowledge.
Then he gave Swaha in marriage to Agni, and the two were never parted.

=== Daksha Cursed Narada | Why Did Daksha Curse Narada to Never Find a Home? ===

Daksha, the son of Brahma, lost six thousand sons. Every single one. And the man who took them never raised a hand.
Daksha had five thousand sons, called the Haryashvas. He told them to rule the Earth and have children. They agreed. Before they set out, they gathered where the River Sindhu meets the sea to make offerings.
Narada, the divine sage, was on his way to them. He asked how they could rule a land they knew nothing about. How would they know if it had too few people or too many? The brothers had no answer. Narada smirked. He told them to learn every detail about the Earth first. They might even find a higher path.
Doubt crept over five thousand faces. The brothers left to explore the Earth. No one saw them again. They never came back.
Daksha and his wife Asikli had another thousand sons, the Shavalashvas. Narada spoke to them in exactly the same way. They vanished too.
Daksha roared. Who would build homes if everyone became a hermit? Narada said he had only shown them a path. It was their choice to walk it.
Daksha cursed him. Narada had scattered his sons across the Earth, so he would wander the three worlds for ever and never find a home.
Was Narada a guide, or a thief of sons?

=== How Vishnu Lost His Head | How Did Vishnu Lose His Head? ===

Vishnu lay asleep, his chin on his bow. Termites were gnawing the bowstring under his face, and every one of them was a god. How did Vishnu lose his head?
Vishnu, the Preserver of the world, had fought one demon for sixteen thousand years. His mace came back and smashed his chariot. The demon laughed. "I have been blessed by a divine one. Only he who knows my secret can slay me."
Vishnu went home, exhausted, and slept. The gods, led by Brahma the Creator, needed him awake for a yagna, a fire ritual, because their own powers had failed against the demon. Brahma had an idea. If the bowstring snapped, the sound would shake the cosmos.
The gods gnawed. Thinner and thinner. Faster and faster.
Twang. The string whipped loose and cut off Vishnu's head. It rolled away. His eyes were still closed. "What have we done?"
Then Lakshmi, his wife, walked in, her feet leaving red prints. "Forgive me," she said. The demon had prayed to her so devotedly that she had blessed him. That was why he was unbeatable. He could die only at the hands of a god with a horse's head.
"That's why! That's why!" Lakshmi nodded. "Get the head of a horse. Sri Hari shall be Hayagriva again."
Soon a horse-headed Vishnu neighed, and the demon fell silent. The gods had beheaded Vishnu by mistake, trying to wake him. And that mistake gave them the one god who could kill the demon.

=== How Shiva Escaped Bhasmasura | How Did Shiva Escape Bhasmasura? ===

Shiva was running for his life. Behind him ran a demon with one hand raised. One touch on Shiva's head, and the Destroyer would be ash. And Shiva himself had given him that power. How did Shiva escape Bhasmasura?
It had begun with a sword at a man's throat. The demon Vrikasura wanted to be Shiva's greatest devotee. The sage Narada told him to win a boon. He prayed for hundreds of years. Shiva stayed hidden. So he began cutting off his own flesh. As the blade reached his neck, Shiva caught his arm.
"Ask," said Shiva. "Anyone I touch on the head turns to ash." "But that is so violent." "If you grant it, it proves you adore me." Shiva agreed. He named him Bhasmasura, the ash demon.
Then Parvati, Shiva's wife, came down the slope. The demon stared. Her husband stood in the way. The demon raised his hand. Shiva stepped back. He ran.
He ran in all ten directions. Every time he looked back, the demon was there. One place was left: Vaikuntha, the home of Vishnu.
Vishnu was smiling. "I know everything, Mahadeva." "Protect me," said Shiva. Vishnu vanished. A woman stood in his place. Mohini. The demon forgot his boon. "Come," she said. "Dance with me."
He copied every step and every turn. Then she placed her hand on her head. So did he.
Mohini laughed as she whirled around a pile of ash. Shiva escaped because Vishnu made the demon dance into his own boon.

=== Why Cut Diti's Unborn Son? | Why Did Indra Cut Diti's Unborn Son Into 49 Pieces? ===

Indra slipped into a sleeping woman's womb, thunderbolt in hand. He cut the unborn child into seven pieces. Then the pieces began to cry. Why did Indra cut Diti's unborn son into forty-nine pieces?
The mother was Diti. Vishnu had killed her sons Hiranyaksha and Hiranyakashipu. The gods, under Indra's command, had slaughtered countless more. She asked her husband, Kashyapa, for a son who would kill Indra. Kashyapa set strict rules. A hundred years of purity. Never lie down without washing her feet. If she failed, her son would befriend Indra.
Indra lurked outside her hut, waiting for one mistake. There was none. Her austerities made her womb a fortress. Her belly grew. His end came closer. Three days remained. He began to write his own elegy.
Then that evening, Diti, exhausted, fell asleep without washing her feet. Indra leapt.
He entered her womb. His Vajra flashed. Seven pieces. He laughed. Then he choked. The pieces were alive. They wailed. "Ma ruda!" he shouted. "Don't cry!" They only grew louder. He cut each piece into seven more. Still alive. Still louder.
Diti woke. Indra fled. She knew she had lost. She gave birth to forty-nine children. They were not his killers. They were his companions, as Kashyapa had decreed. Since Indra had shouted "Ma ruda," they were called the Marutas, the storm gods.
Indra cut the child to stop one killer. He ended with forty-nine allies.

=== How Garuda Freed His Mother | How Did Garuda Free His Mother Vinata From Kadru? ===

At dawn, two sisters stood behind a white horse. Its tail looked black, because snakes were wrapped around it. One sister had just lost her freedom to that trick. How did her son Garuda free his mother Vinata from Kadru?
Kadru and Vinata were sisters, both wives of the sage Kashyapa. Kadru bore a thousand serpents. Vinata wanted two sons mightier than all of them. They argued over a horse. Vinata said Uchchaishrava was white, every inch. Kadru said the tail was black. They bet. The loser would be the winner's slave.
Kadru's sons warned her. "It's white. Every bristle.""White can be black." She ordered them to coil around the tail. Some refused, and she cursed them.
Vinata saw black. She became a slave. Long before, she had cracked open one of her two eggs too early. Out came half-formed Aruna. "You shall be enslaved by your sister," he said. Only the second egg could break the curse.
Five hundred years later, it cracked. Garuda, half bird, half man, rose. "Release my mother." The serpents agreed, for a price. "Bring us amrita, the nectar of immortality."
The nectar sat inside a ring of fire. Blades spun at the entrance. Two serpents waited to strike. Garuda laughed. "Venom is guarding amrita?" He doused the fire with river water, slipped through the blades and trampled the snakes.
He set the vessel down before the serpents. They let Vinata go. Garuda freed his mother by winning the one prize they named.

=== Harishchandra Would Not Break His Word | Why Would Harishchandra Not Break His Word, Even for His Dead Son? ===

Night, at a cremation ground. A king held his dead son in his arms. His wife begged him to break his word, just once. He said no. Why would Harishchandra not break his word, even for his dead son?
He was king of Ayodhya. The god Varuna had once given him a son, on one condition: sacrifice the boy. The king kept finding excuses. Afterwards he swore never to break his word again.
Then a spirit made him insult the sage Vishwamitra. To atone, he promised the sage anything. "Your throne." "All yours." "Your wealth." "All yours."
The sage demanded a fee, dakshina. In Kashi, they found no work. "Sell me," said his wife, Taramati. He refused. "Do it or I shall kill myself." He sold her. It was only half. "Sell yourself too," said the sage. The buyer was a Chandala, a man who burned the dead.
One night a woman came with a dead child and no money for the fee. It was Taramati. The boy was Rohita, bitten by a snake.
"Your word!" she screamed. "You can surely break it for our Rohita." "I did that once," he wept, "and I am still paying." She snatched his axe to end her life.
"Taramati!" Vishwamitra called. Rohita ran to her, alive. He gave back everything. The Chandala was Yama.
He would not break his word because he had broken it once, and sworn never again. That is why his name means truth.

=== Why Paundraka Dressed as Vishnu | Why Did Paundraka Dress as Vishnu to Replace Krishna? ===

A king stood before an army, painted blue, with two wooden arms strapped to his back. He shouted that he was Vishnu. Krishna's soldiers rolled on the ground, laughing. Why did Paundraka dress as Vishnu to replace Krishna?
Paundraka ruled Pundra. Everyone believed Krishna, king of Dwaravati, was Vishnu come to Earth. Paundraka hated him. Now Krishna had killed Paundraka's friend Jarasandh. His flatterers saw their chance. "Krishna is a thief. He steals your glory. You are the true Vishnu." "Am I?" He looked in a mirror.
They said it again and again. "You are Vishnu." Soon he heard only one voice, his own. "I am Vishnu." He renamed himself Vasudeva and sent Krishna a decree. Give up the title, and the disc, mace, bow and conch. Or fight.
Krishna smiled. Then he laughed. "Tell your king I shall battle."
Paundraka marched on Dwaravati. Blue paint. Yellow cloth. A fake gem on his chest. A Garuda banner on his chariot. His own men watched in silence.
Krishna shook with laughter. "A human can never be a god. You are a man behind a mask." "Hand me my weapons," Paundraka snapped. "Take them," said Krishna. "Take them all."
The disc cut off Paundraka's head. The wooden arms fell off. The rain washed away the blue.
"Beware of the Paundrakas of the Earth," said Krishna. "One false god plagues you more than a hundred demons." A lie repeated often enough had made a king believe he was a god.

=== Who Was Gajendra Before? | Who Was Gajendra the Elephant Before Vishnu Saved Him? ===

A crocodile held the leg of Gajendra, king of the elephants. His herd looked at him, then walked away. Alone, bleeding, he lifted a white lotus and prayed to Vishnu. Then a voice called him by another name. Who was Gajendra before Vishnu saved him?
Gajendra had come to a lotus pond with his herd. The crocodile's teeth dug toward the bone. The herd knew a crocodile is stronger in water. Gajendra, devoted to Vishnu, whispered, "I surrender, Sri Hari. There is nothing more I can do. Protect me, or free me if my hour has come."
The morning turned dark. The sun vanished. A blue form spread across the sky. A blue finger held the Sudarshana disc. It flew and cut off the crocodile's head.
Vishnu smiled. "Have you learnt your lesson, Indradyumna?"
Gajendra froze. Indradyumna? The name rippled through his mind like a stone in a pond. Memories rose. He had been a human king. The sage Agastya had come to his court. The king, lost in thought, had not even opened his eyes to greet him. The sage cursed him to be born as an elephant, to learn to surrender.
"The crocodile," Vishnu said, "was a gandharva named Huhu, who insulted the sage Deval. And you?" Gajendra bowed to the ground.
Before he was Gajendra, he was King Indradyumna, too lost in thought to greet a sage. The curse was meant to teach him to surrender. At the pond, he did.

=== Why Garuda Became Vishnu's Mount | Why Did Garuda Become Vishnu's Vahana? ===

Garuda, the half-bird, half-human son of Vinata, was flying off with the nectar of immortality. He had not tasted a drop. Then Vishnu blocked his path. Why did Garuda become Vishnu's vahana, his mount?
The gods had guarded the amrita with fire, spinning blades and two serpents. Garuda broke through all of it, to buy his mother Vinata's freedom from the serpents.
Flying back, he froze. Vishnu was standing in his way. Vishnu had been watching. He knew why Garuda was taking the amrita, and he was pleased that Garuda had not tasted any.
Vishnu offered him a boon. Garuda asked for two things. He wanted to stay above Vishnu. And he wanted to be immortal and free of disease, without drinking the amrita. Vishnu granted both. In return, he asked Garuda to be his carrier, his vahana.
Further on, Indra, king of the gods, hurled his Vajra at Garuda. The mighty weapon shed only one golden feather. Indra saw his strength and stepped aside, but first they made a bargain. Indra promised that serpents would be Garuda's food from then on.
Garuda set the vessel down before the serpents. They freed his mother.
Kadru boasted that her sons served Vishnu. Garuda said he would carry Vishnu too. Vishnu had made him his mount.
That is why Garuda became Vishnu's vahana. He won the nectar for his mother's freedom and never tasted it. Vishnu rewarded him, and Garuda gave his strength in return.
