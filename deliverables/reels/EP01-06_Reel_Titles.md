## 1. Seven Lives of Love or Three of Hate?
- Alternatives: (a) Why Two Guards Chose to Be Vishnu's Enemies; (b) The Curse That Gave a God Two Choices
- Context: Two guards of Vishnu were offered a choice of two fates.
- Lean: Seven lifetimes loving him, or three as his enemies.
- Snapback: They chose hate, and they chose it on purpose.
- Premise: Two gatekeepers turn away four child sages and are cursed to leave Vishnu's home.
- Source: story 08 (EP05)
- Length: about 90 s
- Why: A reversal nobody expects, with a natural comment question.

## 2. He Beat Two Unbeatable Demons by Asking a Favour
- Alternatives: (a) Five Thousand Years of War Ended With One Boon; (b) The Trap Hidden Inside a Boon
- Context: Vishnu fought two brothers for five thousand years and could not win.
- Lean: Then he stopped fighting and offered them a gift.
- Snapback: The gift was a trap, and they chose it themselves.
- Premise: Madhu and Kaitabha steal the Vedas, and Vishnu has to find a way past their strength.
- Source: story 02 (EP01)
- Length: about 90 s
- Why: A clear step-by-step trick with a fast twist ("Grant me that I may kill you both").

## 3. A Sage Went for Water and Lost Everything
- Alternatives: (a) Narada Asked What Maya Is. Vishnu Showed Him; (b) The Thirst That Lasted a Lifetime
- Context: Narada went to fetch water for Vishnu and never brought it.
- Lean: He found a wife, built a home and raised a family.
- Snapback: Vishnu was still waiting, thirsty.
- Premise: A sage asks what maya is and gets an answer he lives through.
- Source: story 07 (EP04)
- Length: about 80 s
- Why: A complete, self-contained twist that works on frame 1.

## 4. How the First Man and Woman Came Into Being
- Alternatives: (a) The God Who Split Himself in Half; (b) Creation Failed Until Shiva Showed Brahma This
- Context: The first humans appeared only after a god showed himself as half male, half female.
- Lean: Brahma had tried everything and failed.
- Snapback: The answer came from the god who had said he could not create.
- Premise: A despairing Creator seeks counsel and finds the secret of creation.
- Source: story 05 (EP03), with a brief reference to story 03
- Length: about 90 s
- Why: A classic origin-of-humanity moment with a striking visual (Ardhanarishwara).

## 5. Shiva Cut Off a Head, Then Paid
- Alternatives: (a) Why Shiva Carried a Skull in His Hand; (b) Brahma Had Five Heads. Why Only Four Now?
- Context: Brahma once had five heads. Now he has four.
- Lean: Shiva cut one off in rage.
- Snapback: But it was Shiva who paid the price.
- Premise: A curse, a severed head and a rule that even a god cannot escape.
- Source: story 06 (EP04)
- Length: about 100 s
- Why: A strong "even gods obey the law" twist that explains why Kashi is holy. Must be told with care and respect.

## 6. A God Born From Rage Demanded a Name
- Alternatives: (a) The First Words of Shiva Were a Demand; (b) Brahma's Anger Gave Birth to a God
- Context: Shiva was born from another god's anger.
- Lean: Brahma had just been refused by his own sons.
- Snapback: The newborn's first demand was not power. It was a name.
- Premise: How Rudra got his name, and why he could not help create.
- Source: story 03 (EP02)
- Length: about 95 s
- Why: A surprising origin of a major god, with a clean question ending.

## 7. (Spare) The Word That Feeds the Gods
- Alternatives: (a) Why Every Offering to the Fire Ends With One Word; (b) The Gods Were Starving While Earth Feasted
- Context: The gods in heaven were starving.
- Lean: Humans were making offerings every day.
- Snapback: The offerings never arrived, and the fire god was to blame.
- Premise: A new goddess solves a cosmic delivery problem.
- Source: story 10 (EP06)
- Length: about 70 s
- Why: A simple problem and solution with a shareable fact (Swaha), though lower in drama.

## 8. (Spare) He Made Six Thousand Princes Vanish
- Alternatives: (a) The Sage Who Emptied a King's House; (b) Daksha's Curse on Narada
- Context: Daksha lost six thousand sons to one conversation.
- Lean: Narada only asked them a question.
- Snapback: He said he had only shown them a path, and Daksha cursed him for it.
- Premise: A father, a wandering sage and a curse that sent the sage onto the road forever.
- Source: story 09 (EP05)
- Length: about 80 s
- Why: A clear cause and effect and a debatable moral, but the last third of story 09 is a genealogy and would need cutting.
