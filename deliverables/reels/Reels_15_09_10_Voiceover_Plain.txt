=== How Garuda Became Vishnu's Vahana | How Did Garuda Become Vishnu's Vahana? ===

Garuda held immortality in his talons. He had not taken a single sip. Then Vishnu stopped him in the sky. How did Garuda become Vishnu's vahana, his mount?
Garuda, half bird and half man, was the son of Vinata. The serpents held her as a slave. They would free her for amrita, the nectar of the gods. The gods had guarded it with fire, spinning blades and two serpents. Garuda broke through all of it.
Flying back, he froze. Vishnu was standing in his way. He knew why Garuda was taking the amrita, and he was pleased that Garuda had not tasted any.
Vishnu offered him a boon. Garuda asked for two things. He wanted to stay above Vishnu. And he wanted to be immortal and free of disease, without drinking the amrita. Vishnu granted both. In return, he asked Garuda to be his carrier, his vahana.
Further on, Indra, king of the gods, hurled his Vajra at Garuda. The mighty weapon shed only one golden feather. Indra saw his strength and stepped aside, but first they made a bargain. Indra promised that serpents would be Garuda's food.
Garuda set the vessel down before the serpents. They freed his mother.
Kadru boasted that her sons served Vishnu. Garuda said he would carry Vishnu too. Vishnu had made him his mount.
That is why Garuda became Vishnu's vahana. He won the nectar for his mother's freedom and never tasted it. Vishnu rewarded him, and Garuda gave his strength in return.

=== Why Shiva Ran From His Devotee | Why Did Shiva Run From His Own Devotee? ===

Shiva said yes to a demon. It was the worst yes of his life. The gift: anyone the demon touched on the head would turn to ash. Then his eyes turned to Shiva's head. Why did Shiva run from his own devotee?
The demon was Vrikasura. He wanted to be Shiva's greatest devotee. The sage Narada told him to win a boon. He prayed for hundreds of years. So he began cutting off his own flesh. As the blade reached his neck, Shiva caught his arm.
"Make my flesh magical," the demon begged. "Anyone I touch on the head turns to ash." "If you grant it, it proves you adore me." Shiva agreed and named him Bhasmasura, the ash demon.
Then Parvati, Shiva's wife, came down the slope. The demon stared. The demon raised his hand. Shiva stepped back. The boon would spare no one, not even him. He ran.
He ran in all ten directions. Every time he looked back, the demon was there. One place was left: Vaikuntha, the home of Vishnu.
"I know everything, Mahadeva." "Protect me," said Shiva. Vishnu vanished. A woman stood in his place. Mohini. The demon forgot his boon. "Come," she said. "Dance with me."
He copied her every step. Then she placed her hand on her head. So did he.
Mohini laughed as she whirled around a pile of ash. Shiva ran because his own gift spared no one. Vishnu saved him by making the demon dance into it.

=== A Son Born to Kill Indra | A Mother Prayed 100 Years for a Son to Kill Indra. How Did Indra Survive? ===

One mother spent a hundred years growing a son to kill a god. Three days were left. Then Indra slipped inside her womb. How did Indra survive?
The mother was Diti. Vishnu had killed her sons Hiranyaksha and Hiranyakashipu. The gods, under Indra's command, had slaughtered countless more. She asked her husband, Kashyapa, for a son who would kill Indra. Kashyapa set strict rules. A hundred years of purity. Never lie down without washing her feet. If she failed, her son would befriend Indra.
Indra lurked outside her hut, waiting for one mistake. There was none. Her austerities made her womb a fortress. Her belly grew. His end came closer. He began to write his own elegy.
Then, three days from the end, Diti, exhausted, fell asleep without washing her feet. Indra leapt.
He entered her womb. His Vajra flashed. Seven pieces. He laughed. Then he choked. The pieces were alive. They wailed. "Ma ruda!" he shouted. "Don't cry!" They only grew louder. He cut each piece into seven more. Still alive. Still louder.
Diti woke. Indra fled. She knew she had lost. She gave birth to forty-nine children. They were not his killers. They were his companions, as Kashyapa had decreed. Since Indra had shouted "Ma ruda," they were called the Marutas, the storm gods.
That is how Indra survived. Diti's son could not be killed, only multiplied, and all forty-nine stood beside him.
