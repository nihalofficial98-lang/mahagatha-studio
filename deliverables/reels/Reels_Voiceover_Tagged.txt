=== Did Shiva Create the First Humans? | Did Shiva Create the First Humans? Brahma Could Not Do It Alone ===

[curious] Everyone says Brahma, the Creator, made the first humans.
[calm] But Brahma could not do it. His own sons had rejected him, and creation was wearing him down.
[curious] So who showed him how? The one god he believed could not create.
[calm] Vishnu, the Preserver, noticed Brahma's grief. He told him to go to Mahadeva, another name for Shiva. Brahma said the Destroyer could not create. Vishnu said Shiva might not create, but he would surely counsel.
Brahma found Shiva meditating. He sat down and prayed from the depth of his soul. Shiva opened his eyes. He said reproduction could not happen the way Brahma had made his sons. What Brahma needed was Shakti, the power of the feminine.
Then Shiva's body changed. The right side became male. The left side became female, split evenly down the middle. This form was called Ardhanarishwara, the Androgynous One.
[firm] Shiva said masculine and feminine were the two halves of the cosmos. Neither could work without the other.
[calm] Brahma bowed to the Goddess and asked to create the female. She gave him the generative force. From his own body, Brahma made a man named Manu and a woman named Brahmi. The human race descends from Manu.
[curious] The Destroyer had not made the first humans. He had shown the Creator what creation needed, both halves.

=== Why Do People Go to Kashi? | Why Do People Go to Kashi? It Started With Shiva and Brahma's Head ===

[curious] Why do people travel to Kashi, also called Varanasi, to be freed of their sins?
[calm] The answer begins with Brahma, the Creator, who once had five heads.
[curious] Shiva cut one off in rage. Then it would not let go of him.
It began with a curse. The sage Narada had said Brahma would desire what must not be desired. When Brahma created his daughter Brahmi, the curse took hold. She saw his gaze and moved away. Each time she moved, a new head grew so he could keep looking at her.
[firm] His sons Marichi and Atri warned him. Brahmi became a cow, a mare, a duck, a doe, a hundred forms in all. She was named Shatarupa.
Shiva appeared in fury. Brahma came to his senses and begged forgiveness. Shiva cut off the fifth head. Brahma's four mouths would recite the Vedas forever, to repent.
[curious] Then Shiva stopped. The severed head was stuck to his left hand.
[calm] Killing a Brahmin was a sin. Atri said every action brings its result, whatever the reason. Brahma had paid, though a curse drove him. So must Shiva.
Shiva wandered the universe, but nothing freed him. The flesh rotted away until only a skull remained. He was called Kapali.
[firm] Then he reached a place on Earth, and the skull fell from his hand. Anyone who came there would be freed of every sin. That place became Kashi, also called Varanasi, and that is why people still go there.

=== Madhu and Kaitabha | How Did Vishnu Defeat Madhu and Kaitabha? ===

[curious] Vishnu, the Preserver, fought two brothers for five thousand years and could not win. Then he put down his mace and offered them a gift. But the gift was never for them.
[calm] The brothers were Madhu and Kaitabha. They crawled out of Vishnu's earwax when Brahma, the Creator, brought forth the Vedas. They fed on the endless water and grew huge.
They found Brahma on his lotus and told him to bow or fight. He stayed silent, so they shook the stem. The Vedas slipped from his hands, and the brothers hid them.
Brahma begged Vishnu, but Vishnu lay in Yoga Nidra, a trance between waking and sleep. Brahma called on Nidra to release him. Vishnu woke as Hayagriva, the Horse-Headed One.
When one brother tired, the other took over. Vishnu saw strength would never win. So he laid down his mace, praised their power, and offered a boon.
[curious] The brothers sneered. Victors did not beg for boons, they said. They gave them. They told him to ask. Vishnu asked, "Grant me that I may kill you both."
[firm] They had walked into a trap, but they had given their word. They asked to die only where there was no water. Vishnu grew, towering over the waters like a blue mountain. He placed the brothers on his thighs and beheaded them.
[calm] For killing Madhu, Vishnu came to be called Madhusudan. Brahma asked him to keep saving them.
[curious] Vishnu said, "Perhaps I did nothing. Perhaps the universe saved itself through me."

=== Narada's Maya Test | Narada Asked Vishnu What Maya Is. Then This Happened ===

[curious] Narada, a great sage, asked Vishnu, the Preserver, what maya is. Vishnu did not explain. He asked for water. Narada never brought it, and that was the answer.
[calm] Narada had come to Vaikuntha, Vishnu's home, to tell him how his curse had come true on his father, Brahma. Vishnu smiled and said it was all maya. Narada asked what that meant.
Vishnu said he was thirsty. He told Narada to fetch some water, and then he would answer.
Narada walked to the river outside. By the bank he saw a young woman. She was so lovely that he forgot Vishnu, his errand and his thirst.
[curious] He married her right there and built a house on the sand by the river.
[calm] Years passed. Narada became a father, then a grandfather. The house had become his whole world.
Then one day the sky turned grey. Thunder roared. Rain poured, and the river flooded. The water swept his family away.
[firm] Suddenly Narada was back in Vaikuntha, standing before Vishnu. Vishnu asked, "Where is the water? I told you I am thirsty."
Narada stood stunned. His wife, his children, his home. None of it had been real.
Vishnu said that was maya. Attachment to a world that does not last. It makes us chase what slips away like sand.
[curious] Then he smiled and warned Narada that there would be much more maya to come.

=== Shiva Born From Anger | How Was Shiva Born From Brahma's Anger? ===

[curious] Shiva was born from another god's anger. His first demand was not for power. It was for a name.
[calm] Brahma, the Creator, had made four sons from his mind. They were child sages called the Kumaras. He told them to help him fill the world with living beings. They folded their hands and said no.
They said they were grateful, but they did not want to be bound by life and death. They wanted to meditate on the infinite. They asked only that they never grow old. Brahma argued, then gave in. As his sons left, rage rose in him.
[curious] A fiery beam burst from his forehead. A being stepped out of it with blazing red eyes. He roared, "What is my name?"
[calm] Brahma sat in awe. The being roared again. "Tell me my name." Brahma remembered that the root word for both red and roaring is Rud. He said, "Rudra. I name you Rudra."
This was Shiva, also called Mahadeva. Brahma created, Vishnu preserved, and Shiva was the Destroyer.
Brahma asked him to help with creation. Shiva agreed and began making beings. But he made copies of himself, and each was immortal. Eleven Rudras stood before Brahma.
[firm] Brahma said creation needed mortals, who die and are born again. Shiva said he could only make immortals. If that spoiled the plan, he would rather meditate. Then he left.
[curious] The god born from Brahma's anger could only make what never dies.

=== Why Say Swaha? | Why Does Every Offering to Agni End With Swaha? ===

[curious] Every offering to the fire god Agni ends with the word Swaha. The gods once starved while humans fed the fire regularly. The fire god himself said it was his fault.
[calm] One morning, the gods came to Brahma, the Creator, in Brahmaloka. They were thin and weak. They had nothing to eat.
Brahma explained how they were meant to eat. When humans held a yagna, a fire ritual, they poured offerings into the fire. Agni, the mouth of the gods, carried them to heaven. In return, the gods gave humans blessings.
[curious] Indra, a leader of the gods, said humans held yagnas all the time. But nothing reached heaven.
[calm] Another god, Kamadeva, blamed Agni. Agni stepped forward with folded hands. He said he was to blame. He could burn the offerings, but he could not carry them to the gods. He did not have that skill.
Brahma had no answer. Vishnu, the Preserver, told him to pray to Prakriti, Mother Nature.
Prakriti created a young goddess to carry offerings from Earth to heaven. Brahma asked her name.
[firm] "Swaha," she said.
Brahma declared that every offering to Agni must end with her name. Without it, the offering would not reach the gods. A chant without Swaha would be like a tree without fruit, a snake without venom, a man without knowledge.
[curious] Then he gave Swaha in marriage to Agni, and the two were never parted.

=== Daksha Cursed Narada | Why Did Daksha Curse Narada to Never Find a Home? ===

[curious] Daksha, the son of Brahma, lost six thousand sons. Every single one. And the man who took them never raised a hand.
[calm] Daksha had five thousand sons, called the Haryashvas. He told them to rule the Earth and have children. They agreed. Before they set out, they gathered where the River Sindhu meets the sea to make offerings.
Narada, the divine sage, was on his way to them. [curious] He asked how they could rule a land they knew nothing about. How would they know if it had too few people or too many? The brothers had no answer. Narada smirked. He told them to learn every detail about the Earth first. They might even find a higher path.
Doubt crept over five thousand faces. The brothers left to explore the Earth. No one saw them again. They never came back.
Daksha and his wife Asikli had another thousand sons, the Shavalashvas. Narada spoke to them in exactly the same way. They vanished too.
[firm] Daksha roared. Who would build homes if everyone became a hermit? Narada said he had only shown them a path. It was their choice to walk it.
Daksha cursed him. Narada had scattered his sons across the Earth, so he would wander the three worlds for ever and never find a home.
[curious] Was Narada a guide, or a thief of sons?

=== Vishnu Lost His Head | Vishnu Fought a Demon for 16,000 Years. Then He Lost His Head. ===

[curious] Vishnu fought one demon for sixteen thousand years and could not beat him. Then the gods cut off Vishnu's head. So who could kill a demon no god could beat?
[calm] Nothing worked on the demon. Vishnu's mace was thrown back and smashed his chariot. His Sudarshana disc stopped at an invisible wall. The demon laughed. Only one who knew his secret could kill him.
[calm] Worn out, Vishnu went home to Vaikuntha and fell into a deep sleep, his chin on his bow. The gods, led by Brahma, could not wake him.
[calm] Brahma had an idea. Vishnu was sleeping on his bowstring. If it snapped, the sound would wake him. The gods turned into termites and gnawed at the string. Thinner and thinner. Faster and faster. Then it snapped.
[firm] The string lashed out and cut off Vishnu's head. Silence. The gods stood frozen. What have we done?
[curious] Then Lakshmi, Vishnu's wife, walked in, leaving red footprints. She said the demon's boon was hers. His devotion had touched her. She had blessed him that only a god with a horse's head could kill him.
[calm] That was the answer. The gods looked at the headless Vishnu. Lakshmi said the universe is never random. It looks after itself. Vishnu would return as Hayagriva, the Horse-Headed One.
[calm] Not long after, Vishnu as Hayagriva, the Horse-Headed One, neighed aloud, and the demon fell silent.
[firm] The one god who could kill the unbeatable demon was Vishnu, with the head of a horse.

=== Shiva Ran From a Devotee | Shiva Ran From His Own Devotee ===

[curious] A demon asked Shiva for a boon that could turn anyone to ash. Shiva said yes, and the demon chased Shiva himself. How do you escape a boon even a god cannot stop?
[calm] The demon was Vrikasura, a devotee of Shiva, the Destroyer. Narada, the trouble-maker sage, told him he was no true devotee without a boon. He prayed on Mount Kailash for hundreds of years. Shiva did not come. He began cutting off parts of his body as offerings. As his sword reached his neck, Shiva caught his arm and healed him.
[calm] Vrikasura asked that anyone he touched on the head turn to ashes. Shiva called it violent. Vrikasura said it would prove Shiva's love. Shiva agreed and named him Bhasmasura, the ash demon.
[firm] Then Parvati, Shiva's wife, came down the slope. Bhasmasura stared. He could not have her while her husband lived. He raised his hand toward Shiva's head.
[curious] Shiva ran. The boon would not spare even him. He ran in all ten directions, but the demon was always right behind. At last he reached Vaikuntha, the home of Vishnu.
[calm] Vishnu vanished, and a woman stood in his place.
[calm] She was Mohini. Bhasmasura forgot everything, even his boon. She said, "Come. Dance with me." He copied every step and every turn.
[firm] Then she placed her hand on her head. So did he. Mohini laughed as she whirled around a pile of ash.
[curious] That is how Shiva escaped. Vishnu, as Mohini, made the demon turn himself to ash.

=== Indra Cut an Unborn Child | Indra Tried to Kill an Unborn Child. It Backfired. ===

[curious] Indra, king of the gods, once entered a mother's womb to kill her unborn son. That son was growing up to be his killer. So how did his plan backfire?
[calm] Diti was the mother of the demons. Vishnu had killed her sons Hiranyaksha and Hiranyakashipu. The gods under Indra had killed many more. She asked her husband, Kashyapa, for a son who would kill Indra.
[calm] Kashyapa agreed, with strict rules. The child would grow in her womb for a hundred years. All that time she must live as an ascetic and never lie down without washing her feet. If she failed, her son would befriend Indra instead.
[firm] Diti followed every rule. Indra watched for years, waiting for one mistake. She made none. Then only three days remained.
[curious] That evening, Diti was tired. She fell asleep without washing her feet. Indra leapt in. He entered her womb and cut the child into seven pieces with his Vajra, his thunderbolt.
[firm] The pieces were alive. They wailed. Indra shouted, "Ma ruda," which means, "Don't cry." They only grew louder. He cut each piece into seven more. They still would not die.
[calm] Diti woke and Indra vanished. She had lost. She gave birth to forty-nine children. They were no threat to Indra. They became his companions, as Kashyapa had said. Because of his cry, they were called the Marutas, the storm gods.
[curious] That is how it backfired. Indra wanted one killer gone. He got forty-nine friends.

=== Kadru Cheated Vinata | Kadru Cheated Her Sister Vinata, and Garuda Paid for It ===

[curious] Kadru cheated her sister Vinata in a bet over a horse's tail. The loser became a slave. So why did Vinata's son have to pay?
[calm] Kadru, mother of a thousand serpents, and her sister Vinata were wives of the sage Kashyapa. Vinata's two eggs would not hatch. Impatient, she cracked one open. Inside was half-formed Aruna. He cursed her to become her sister's slave, unless the other egg hatched on its own.
[calm] The ocean had been churned, and a white horse, Uchchaishrava, rose from it. Vinata said it was white to the tail. Kadru said the tail was black. The loser would be the winner's slave.
[firm] Kadru's sons told her the tail was white. She ordered them to coil around it so no white hair showed. Some refused, and she cursed them. At dawn the tail looked black. Vinata admitted defeat and became a slave.
[calm] Five hundred years later, the second egg hatched. Garuda, half bird and half man, demanded his mother's freedom. The serpents set a price: amrita, the nectar of immortality. Garuda broke through fire, spinning blades and guard snakes and brought it.
[firm] The serpents freed Vinata. Indra swooped in and took the nectar back. A few drops fell on sharp grass. The serpents licked them and split their tongues. That is why snakes have forked tongues.
[curious] That is how Garuda paid for Kadru's cheating. He won his mother's freedom, and Vishnu made him his mount. Vinata smiled.

=== He Lost Everything | King Harishchandra Lost His Kingdom, His Wife and His Son ===

[curious] King Harishchandra lost his kingdom, his wife and his son. He gave each one up himself. Why would a king give up everything?
[calm] Harishchandra, king of Ayodhya, had once broken a vow to the water god Varuna. He swore never again. One day a spirit made him insult the sage Vishwamitra. To make amends, he promised the sage anything. Vishwamitra took his throne, his wealth and Ayodhya.
[calm] The king left with his wife, Taramati, and son, Rohita. The sage demanded dakshina, a payment for accepting charity. The family found no work in Kashi. Taramati asked to be sold as a maid. Harishchandra refused until she threatened to die. It was only half.
[firm] Vishwamitra told him to sell himself to a Chandala, a man who burned the dead. He worked at the cremation ground.
[curious] One night, a woman came with her dead son and no money for the fee. Harishchandra said he could not help. She was Taramati. The boy was Rohita, bitten by a snake.
[firm] She begged him to break his word just once. He refused. He had given his word to his master, and the gods were watching. She grabbed an axe to end her life.
[calm] Then Vishwamitra called her name. Rohita ran to her alive. The sage said he could not defeat the king, and gave back everything. The Chandala was Yama, the god of death.
[curious] Harishchandra lost everything because he would not break his word. That is why his name stands for truth.

=== A King Played Vishnu | A King Dressed as Vishnu to Replace Krishna ===

[curious] King Paundraka dressed up as Vishnu to replace Krishna. He painted himself blue and strapped on wooden arms. Why would a king make himself look so foolish?
[calm] Paundraka ruled the eastern land of Pundra. He hated Krishna, the Yadava king of Dwaravati. When Krishna killed his friend Jarasandh, Paundraka raged. His flatterers told him Krishna was a thief, and that Paundraka was the true Vishnu.
[calm] They said it again and again. Paundraka believed it. He renamed himself Vasudeva, a name of Vishnu, and sent Krishna a decree. Give up the title and the divine weapons, or fight.
[firm] Krishna smiled, then laughed. He said he would fight.
[curious] Paundraka marched on Dwaravati. His body was blue and his cloth was yellow. Two wooden arms were strapped behind his own to look four-armed. A Garuda banner flew over his chariot.
[calm] Krishna's soldiers laughed. Krishna said gods create humans, but a human can never be a god. Paundraka was only a man behind a mask.
[firm] Paundraka snapped. He called Krishna the fraud and demanded his weapons. Krishna said, "Take them. Take them all." He hurled his conch, bow, mace and disc.
[calm] The disc cut off Paundraka's head. His wooden arms fell off. The rain washed the blue paint off his skin.
[curious] Krishna warned everyone to beware of the Paundrakas of the Earth. One false god, he said, would plague people more than a hundred demons. A lie told again and again had made a king believe he was a god.

=== His Herd Left Him | An Elephant Called Out to Vishnu While a Crocodile Held His Leg ===

[curious] A crocodile clamped onto the leg of Gajendra, king of the elephants. His whole herd walked away. Then Vishnu came and called him by another name. Why?
[calm] By the Ocean of Milk was a lotus pond where elephants came to drink. Their leader was Gajendra, a king tusker devoted to Vishnu.
[firm] One day, Gajendra trumpeted in terror. A crocodile had seized his leg. Blood clouded the pond. The herd knew a crocodile is stronger in water. They watched helplessly, and then they left.
[calm] Gajendra was alone. He stopped fighting. He lifted a white lotus in his trunk and prayed to Vishnu. He said he could do nothing to save himself. Protect me, he said, or free me if my hour has come.
[curious] The morning turned dark. A blue shape spread across the sky, a blue finger holding the Sudarshana disc. The disc cut off the crocodile's head.
[calm] Vishnu smiled. He asked, "Have you learnt your lesson, Indradyumna?"
[firm] Gajendra froze. The name stirred a past life. He had been a king named Indradyumna. When the sage Agastya came to his court, he did not even open his eyes to greet him. The sage cursed him to be born as an elephant, to learn to surrender.
[calm] The crocodile, Vishnu said, was a gandharva named Huhu, who had insulted the sage Deval. Gajendra bowed to the ground.
[curious] That is why Vishnu called him Indradyumna. The elephant was the cursed king, and he had finally learned to surrender.
