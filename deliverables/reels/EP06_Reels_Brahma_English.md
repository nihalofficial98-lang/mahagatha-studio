## REEL 1 - "The God Who Has Almost No Temples" (ending: twist)
Hook: Context: Brahma has almost no temples. | Lean: one lie and a flower. | Snapback: the flower was forced to tell it.

The god who created the universe has almost no temples. It's because of one lie, and a flower that was forced to tell it.

This story comes from the Puranas. Brahma and Vishnu were arguing over who was greater. Suddenly, a pillar of fire appeared between them. It had no top and no bottom. The gods set a challenge. Vishnu would find its root. Brahma would find its peak. Whoever returned first would win.

Vishnu became a boar and dug down. Brahma became a swan and flew up. Neither found an end. Vishnu came back and admitted he had failed. Brahma had failed too. But a Ketaki flower drifted down from above, and he saw his chance. He ordered it to testify that he had reached the peak. It hesitated. Then it agreed.

Brahma claimed victory. The flower backed him. Then the pillar split open, and Shiva appeared. The pillar was Shiva himself. "Liar," he said.

From that day on, no one would worship Brahma. And no one would ever offer the Ketaki flower to Shiva.

## REEL 2 - "Shiva Cursed Brahma. Well, Not Really." (ending: respect restored)
Hook: Context: Shiva "cursed" Brahma. | Lean: that is why Brahma isn't worshipped. | Snapback: Shiva himself said it was not a curse.

Shiva cursed Brahma so that no one would worship him. Well, not really.

We've all heard it that way. But in the story, Shiva says it himself: "This is not a curse. This is the law of nature."

Here is what happened. Brahma lied to win a contest against Vishnu. A Ketaki flower was his false witness. Then Shiva rose from a pillar of fire and exposed the lie. He said something strange. "Hari accepted his defeat. But you were so afraid of losing that you cheated."

Then he explained the result. You denied the truth, so the world will deny you. It was not a punishment born of anger. It was a consequence.

That is why Brahma's temples are so rare today. But it doesn't erase what he did. He created the universe. His mistake wasn't evil. It was the fear of losing.

## REEL 3 - "Brahma Lied. The Flower Paid For It." (ending: open question for comments)
Hook: Context: Brahma lied to win. | Lean: someone was punished alongside him. | Snapback: a flower.

Brahma lied to win. But the one punished along with him was a flower.

It started with a race to find the end of an endless pillar of fire. Brahma flew up as a swan and found nothing. Then a Ketaki flower drifted down from above. It said it had slipped from the pillar's heights. Brahma heard only two words: the heights.

He asked the flower to testify that he had reached the peak. The flower said, "You want me to lie?" Brahma said, "I created you. I'm asking for one favour." After a long silence, it agreed.

Then Shiva appeared and exposed everything. To Brahma he said, no one will worship you. To the flower he said, "You were dear to me. But you gave false testimony. No one will offer you to me again."

Brahma lost his worship. The Ketaki lost its place at Shiva's feet. So who was more guilty? The one who asked for the lie, or the one who agreed to it?
