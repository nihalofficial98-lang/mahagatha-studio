# EP06 - 3 standalone reels (English). Each is a complete story, no loose ends.
A = Vishnu's view, B = the flower's view, C = Brahma's view. Facts only from episodes/ep06/script.md. Triple hook first; one closing line; no "next reel".

---
## REEL A - "The God Who Won by Losing"
Context: Vishnu lost a contest, and admitted it. | Lean: that loss made him the greatest. | Snapback: the one who "won" is still paying for it.

**A1** [fast, mysterious]
Vishnu lost a contest. And he admitted it himself.
That one loss made him the greatest of all.
The god who "won" that day is still paying for it.

**A2** [slow, measured]
Brahma creates. Vishnu protects. One day they clashed: who is greater?
Brahma said, "I am the creator. Without me, you'd have nothing to protect."
Vishnu said, "And who keeps creation alive? I killed Madhu and Kaitabha."
So each invited the other inside himself. Brahma held a million worlds. Vishnu held countless universes.
They were equal. And that was the whole problem.
Neither would admit it. Weapons were raised.

**A3** [grand, tense]
Then, between them, a pillar of fire erupted. Blazing like gold. No top. No bottom.
The gods set a challenge. Vishnu would find the root. Brahma, the peak. First to arrive is greater.
Vishnu became a boar and tore down through the earth.
Down. And further down. The pillar never ended.
Then Vishnu stopped. How do you find the root of something endless?

**A4** [calm, firm]
He turned back. Head bowed. Empty-handed.
Brahma was already there, smiling. He claimed he had reached the peak, touched it, and returned the winner.
Vishnu hid nothing. "I lost. I could not find the root."
And he folded his hands.

**A5** [explosive, then grave]
Then the pillar split. The sound of Om rolled out. Shiva stepped from the flames.
"This was never a contest. This is the Jyotirlinga, endless like Parabrahma."
"I came only to show you how small this quarrel is."
He turned to Brahma. "You lied to win."
Then to Vishnu. "Hari accepted his defeat."

**A6 - closing line** [calm]
Someone who can accept defeat never needs a false victory. Vishnu lost that day. And right there, he became the greatest.
Have you ever lost and come out ahead? Tell me in the comments.

## REEL B - "Why Is the Ketaki Flower Never Offered to Shiva?"
Context: The Ketaki flower is never offered to Shiva. | Lean: the reason is one false testimony. | Snapback: the flower never wanted to lie.

**B1** [mysterious, fast]
The Ketaki flower is never offered to Shiva. The reason: one false testimony.
But the truth is, that flower never wanted to lie.

**B2** [thrilling, slow]
Brahma and Vishnu were racing to find the end of an endless pillar of fire. First one there wins.
Vishnu dove down. Brahma flew up as a swan.
He flew. He tired. There was no peak anywhere.
Then something fell from above. A Ketaki flower.

**B3** [slow, dangerous]
Brahma caught it. "Where are you coming from?"
"From the heights of this pillar. I slipped."
Brahma barely heard the sentence. Two words stuck: the heights.
In that moment the flower stopped being a flower. It became the key to victory.

**B4** [tense, pressing]
"You will testify before everyone. You will say I reached the peak."
The Ketaki shuddered. "You want me to lie?"
"I created you. I ask just one favour in return."
"But a lie?"
"Stand with me, and I will prove my greatness."
A long silence. Then the Ketaki agreed.

**B5** [cunning, then triumphant]
Below, Vishnu had already returned. "I lost. I couldn't reach the root."
Brahma smiled and held out the flower. And the flower spoke: "Lord Brahma is the greatest."
The lie worked. Vishnu folded his hands.

**B6** [explosive, grave]
Then the pillar of fire cracked. Shiva appeared. "Liar!"
He turned to the flower. "You were dear to me. But you gave false testimony."
"From today, no one will offer you to me."

**B7 - closing line** [calm, still]
And from that day to this, the Ketaki flower has never reached Shiva's feet.
Not only the liar pays. The one who helps him lie pays too.
If someone powerful asked you to lie for them, could you say no?

## REEL C - "Why Does No One Worship Brahma?"
Context: Brahma created the universe, yet no one worships him. | Lean: it looks like Shiva's curse. | Snapback: Shiva himself said, "This is not a curse."

**C1** [fast, mysterious]
Brahma created the entire universe. Yet no one worships him.
Everyone says it was Shiva's curse. But Shiva himself said, "This is not a curse."
So what was it?

**C2** [grand, challenging]
Brahma is the creator. But one day a thorn pricked his mind. Am I the greatest?
He argued with Vishnu. Each showed the whole universe inside himself, and they came out equal.
Then an endless pillar of fire appeared. No top. No bottom.
The challenge was set: whoever finds its end first is greater. Vishnu went down. Brahma flew up as a swan.

**C3** [slow, dangerous]
Brahma flew on, beyond the clouds, and higher. No peak.
Just as he turned back, a Ketaki flower fell from above. "I come from the heights of this pillar."
And one thought flashed through Brahma's mind: I cannot lose.
"Testify before everyone that I reached the peak."
The flower hesitated. Then it agreed.

**C4** [cunning, then explosive]
Below, Vishnu told the truth: "I lost."
Brahma smiled and held out the flower. The flower said, "Brahma is the greatest."
Then the pillar split in two. Shiva appeared. "Liar!"

**C5** [grave, heavy]
"This pillar is the Shivalinga, endless like Parabrahma. No one can see its peak."
"Hari accepted defeat. But Brahma, you feared losing so much that you cheated."
"One who is full of fear himself, how can he remove the fear of others?"
Shiva struck his trident on the ground.
"This is not a curse. This is the law of nature. You denied the truth. Now all will deny you."
"From today, no one will worship you."

**C6 - closing line** [calm, deep]
That is why, even today, Brahma's temples are rare.
The one who made the universe was beaten by one defeat. The hunger to win, and the fear of losing. That was his mistake.
If you were Brahma, would you have told the truth?
