## Reel 1: How the First Man and Woman Came Into Being (story 05) - FIX (1 blocking)

Hook as written:
- Context: "The first man and woman appeared only after a god showed himself as half male and half female."
- Lean: "Brahma, the Creator, had tried everything and failed."
- Snapback: "The answer came from Shiva, the Destroyer, the one god Brahma believed could not create."

1. Facts: all traceable except "had tried everything". The source gives only two failures: the sons who rejected him and Shiva breeding immortals. "Tried everything" is overstated.
2. Spoken English: natural. No awkward lines.
3. Hook: it gives the answer away. Line 1 tells the whole payoff (half male, half female) and line 3 names Shiva, so nothing is left to wait for. Line 2 is a vague lean.
4. Standalone: ok. Brahma, Vishnu, Shiva, Mahadeva, Shakti and Ardhanarishwara are all introduced.
5. Ending: resolved ("both halves"). No pointer. OK.
6. Title: honest, name in title, "How ..." format. OK.
7. Words: 231, OK. 8. Tags: OK. 9. Not a sensitive reel. The Brahmi curse is left out, which is right.

**Blocking fix 1 (hook gives the answer, "tried everything" overstated).** Replace the first three narration lines with the following. It still has context (Brahma cannot make the first humans), a concrete lean (his sons rejected him), and a snapback (the Destroyer helps). The Ardhanarishwara reveal stays for the body.

> [curious] Brahma, the Creator, could not make the first man and woman.
> [calm] His own sons had rejected him, and creation was wearing him down.
> [curious] The one who helped was Shiva, the Destroyer, the very god Brahma believed could not create.

Net result: 217 words. If the owner wants about 230, restore this sentence after "Mahadeva, another name for Shiva.": "Brahma said the Destroyer could not create." (already present, no change).

Should fix (not blocking): none.

## Reel 2: Brahma Had 5 Heads. Why Only Four Now? (story 06) - FIX (2 blocking)

Hook as written:
- Context: "Brahma once had five heads. Now he has four."
- Lean: "Shiva cut one off in rage."
- Snapback: "But it was Shiva who paid the price."

1. Facts: traceable. Checked: Narada's curse, heads at each move, Marichi and Atri, cow/mare/duck/doe and a hundred forms, Shatarupa, fifth head severed, "four mouths ... Vedas to repent", head on left hand, Brahmahatya, Atri's rule, rotting flesh, Kapali, skull falls, Kashi/Varanasi. No invented details. Nothing graphic added.
2. Spoken English: one awkward, unclear line: "Brahma had paid, though he was cursed. Shiva must pay too." It is ambiguous (paid what, for what). Fix below.
3. Hook: works as a triple hook. Weak spot: Brahma is not introduced in line 1 (a stranger does not know who he is).
4. Standalone: Brahma has no role in line 1; Shiva has none at "Shiva appeared in fury" (see fix 2). Narada, Marichi, Atri are introduced well enough ("sage", "His sons").
5. Ending: resolved with Kashi. No pointer. OK. Manu and Shatarupa's closing lament is correctly left out.
6. Title: honest, striking fact plus question. OK.
7. Words: 248, at the limit. Both fixes below keep it within 250.
8. Tags: OK.
9. Restraint: well handled. The curse and the daughter are named once, with no description of desire. "Brahma followed in the male form each time" is mild and acceptable. Nothing to soften further. Keep "Brahma came to his senses and begged forgiveness".

**Blocking fix 1 (unclear line).** Replace
"Brahma had paid, though he was cursed. Shiva must pay too."
with
> Brahma had paid, though a curse drove him. So must Shiva.

(Reads aloud as two plain beats: Brahma paid even though a curse drove him; so must Shiva. Same facts as Atri's line in the source.)

**Blocking fix 2 (stranger test: Brahma and Shiva have no roles in the first lines).** Replace line 1
"Brahma once had five heads. Now he has four."
with
> Brahma, the Creator, once had five heads. Now he has four.

and delete the sentence "Soon he had five." (the title and line 1 already say five). Net result: 246 words.

Should fix (not blocking): "Killing a Brahmin was a sin" leaves a stranger asking why Brahma counts. Optional: "Killing Brahma counted as Brahmahatya, the sin of killing a Brahmin." (the source uses the word; +5 words, 251, so only if "Then Shiva stopped." is cut).

## Reel 3: How Did Vishnu Defeat Madhu and Kaitabha? (story 02) - FIX (2 blocking)

- Lean: "Then he put down his mace and offered them a gift."
- Snapback: none in the narration. The beats file says "The gift was a trap, and the brothers walked into it themselves", but that line is not spoken.

Also "Vishnu smiled" at the growth: the source smile is at "Hayagriva smiled again", fine.
2. Spoken English: fine. "Victors did not beg for boons, they said. They gave them." is clear.
3. Hook: has context and lean in line 1-2 but no snapback. Also Vishnu has no role until later ("Vishnu, the Preserver" appears only at the Yoga Nidra beat).
4. Standalone: Brahma, Vishnu, Hayagriva, Yoga Nidra are introduced. OK once Vishnu's role moves up.
5. Ending: open question quote, story complete, no pointer. OK.
6. Title: honest, name in title, question format. OK.
7. Words: 248. 8. Tags: OK. 9. Not applicable.

**Blocking fix 1 (hook has no snapback; Vishnu unintroduced).** Replace the first two narration sentences
"Vishnu fought two brothers for five thousand years and could not win. Then he put down his mace and offered them a gift."

and change "Brahma begged Vishnu, the Preserver, but Vishnu lay in Yoga Nidra" to

Delete "Five thousand years passed." (now repeated), and in "So he laid down his mace" keep as is. Also change "Vishnu saw that strength would never win." to "Vishnu saw strength would never win." and "Vishnu smiled and grew, towering" to "Vishnu grew, towering".

**Blocking fix 2 (overstated).** Replace

with

Net result with both fixes: 249 words (at the top of the range; if the owner wants slack, also cut "praised their power,").

Should fix (not blocking): "He placed the brothers on his thighs" is unexplained to a stranger (why is that "no water"). The source does not say it either; the story's logic is that he towered above the water.
