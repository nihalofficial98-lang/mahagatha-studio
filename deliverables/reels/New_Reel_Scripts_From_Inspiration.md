# New reel scripts from the shared inspiration videos (11 stories)

Rules followed: the title is the first line, then the most shocking fact. Simple plain English, no idioms, narration only (no quoted dialogue), "ashram" not "hermitage". Each is about 150 to 190 words (about 70 to 85 seconds).
These are our own retellings, not the original wording. Facts that come only from the source video and that I could not check are marked **(check)**. Please confirm them before recording.

## 1. Why Didn't Parashurama Kill Ravana?
Source: the two Parashurama SRTs.

**Title:** Why Didn't Parashurama Kill Ravana?

Parashurama cut off the thousand arms of the king who once captured Ravana. And he still never fought Ravana. Why?

Ravana had a boon that kept gods and demons from killing him. He never asked for safety from humans. Parashurama was a human. So the boon was not the reason.

Parashurama was not too weak, either. And no verse says he spared Ravana because Ravana was born a Brahmin.

The truth is simpler. Parashurama had already finished his work. He had given the whole earth to the sage Kashyapa and gone to live on Mount Mahendra.

Later, he met young Rama on the road after Rama broke Shiva's bow. Parashurama held out the bow of Vishnu and told Rama to string it. Rama did. Then Rama aimed the arrow at the worlds Parashurama had earned by his penance. Parashurama asked him to take those worlds. He said there was no shame in losing to Vishnu himself.

The gods had asked Vishnu to be born as Dasharatha's son to kill Ravana. That job was given to Rama. It was never meant for Parashurama.

## 2. Did Kumbhakarna Really Make Hanuman Vomit Blood?
Source: audio "Hanuman ji vs Kumbhakaran".

**Title:** Did Kumbhakarna Really Hurt Hanuman?

Kumbhakarna hit Hanuman so hard with a spear that Hanuman threw up blood.

It happened in the war in Lanka. Hanuman threw huge mountain peaks at Kumbhakarna. But the giant did not fall. He drove his spear into Hanuman's chest.

The Valmiki Ramayana says Hanuman was badly wounded, with blood pouring from his mouth. But he was not defeated.

Later, Kumbhakarna threw his spear at Sugriva. Hanuman jumped in front of it, caught it, and broke it across his knee.

Then Kumbhakarna grabbed Sugriva. Hanuman thought about growing as large as a mountain and crushing the giant with his fists. But he trusted Sugriva to escape on his own. And Sugriva did.

Kumbhakarna kept fighting until he faced Rama. Rama cut off his arms, then his legs, and at last cut off his head with a divine arrow.

## 3. Is Ashwatthama Still Alive?
Source: audio "Is Ashwathama really alive". All of this is told as what local people believe.

**Title:** Is Ashwatthama Still Alive?

Every morning, before anyone arrives, fresh flowers are already lying on a Shivling at a temple in Madhya Pradesh. People say Ashwatthama put them there.

The temple is the Gupteshwar Mahadev Temple, inside the Asirgarh Fort. Local people say that Ashwatthama, the warrior who lived through the Kurukshetra war, still comes here to worship Shiva.

There is another story from the banks of the Narmada river. Travelers say a very tall stranger comes to them. He has a wound on his forehead that never heals. He asks for oil or turmeric to ease the pain.

People say this is Ashwatthama, wandering for thousands of years after the war. In these stories he does not ask for money or power. He only asks for something to ease his pain.

But these are stories that people pass down. What do the old books really say? Did Krishna truly curse him to live forever?

## 4. When Will Vishnu's Last Avatar Come?
Source: audio "Kalki Avatar" (Hindi). I wrote this one in English; tell me if you want it in Hindi.

**Title:** When Will Vishnu's Last Avatar Come?

The Vishnu Purana names the village where the last avatar will be born. It also names his father.

The village is Shambhala. His father is a Brahmin named Vishnuyasha. The child will be Kalki.

The same book describes the age we live in, the Kali Yuga. A person will be respected for money, not for goodness. People will do religion only to show others. Relatives will stay together only for gain. And people will win in court with lies and cheat their own family for money.

Which of these is not true today?

But the Purana also says what comes at the end. When evil goes beyond every limit, Vishnu will be born as Kalki, with divine power. People's minds will become clear, like crystal, as if they have woken after a long night. And when the moon, Jupiter and a star called Pushya come together, a new golden age will begin.

The last part is the kindest. In earlier ages, people needed years of meditation, or great fires, or long worship. In the Kali Yuga, saying God's name is enough.

## 5. Did Indra Really Almost Capture Ravana?
Source: audio "Ravan Lost to Indra in the Battle". **(check)** The first half, where Indra tries to capture Ravana, comes from the source video. The part where Meghanada captures Indra is in the Valmiki Ramayana (Uttara Kanda).

**Title:** Did Indra Almost Defeat Ravana?

Ravana, the king who beat many gods, was once trapped by Indra's army. He could not get out.

Ravana had decided to attack heaven. He wanted to take Indra's kingdom. At first, both armies fought hard.

Then Indra decided to take Ravana alive. His army surrounded the demon king. For the first time, Ravana was struggling just to escape.

At that moment, Ravana's son Meghanada entered the battle. Meghanada knew he could not beat Indra in an open fight. So he used his magic to become invisible, and attacked from places Indra could not see.

In the end, he overpowered Indra and took him to Lanka as a prisoner. This is why people call Meghanada by a new name, Indrajit, the one who won over Indra.

The matter became so serious that Brahma himself came to Lanka and asked for Indra's release.

## 6. Did Ravana Really Never Touch Sita?
Source: audio "Ravan was not evil" and the Hindi audio about Ravana's mistake. This is shown without graphic detail.

**Title:** Did Ravana Really Never Touch Sita?

Ravana did not hurt Sita. But it was not because he was a good man. It was because a curse would have split his head into a hundred pieces.

People say this proves Ravana was great. The old books say something different.

Hanuman, when he entered Ravana's palace, saw rooms filled with women. The Ramayana says Ravana had taken them during his wars.

Ravana had also attacked an apsara named Punjikasthala. When Brahma learned of it, he cursed Ravana. If Ravana ever forced himself on a woman again, his head would split into a hundred pieces. Another telling says the curse came from Nalakubara, after Ravana attacked the apsara Rambha.

When Ravana took Sita away, he grabbed her by force. And in the Yuddha Kanda, he himself admits why he never forced her into his bed. He did not respect her. He feared the curse.

A king who fears only a curse is not a great man.

## 7. Did Hanuman Ever Use a Mace?
Source: audio "Shree Hanuman never used Gada in a Battle". The source ends with "I will tell you in the next video." This version ends differently.

**Title:** Did Hanuman Ever Fight With a Mace?

In Valmiki's Ramayana, Hanuman never fights with a mace.

We always see him carrying a huge one. But in the Sundara Kanda, when Ravana sent thousands of demons to kill him, Hanuman tore a heavy iron bar out of a gateway. He used that bar to destroy them.

When Ravana sent the seven sons of his ministers, Hanuman fought them with his bare hands. He struck some with his palms and some with his feet.

So in battle he used his fists, his nails, his chest and his legs. Some enemies fell to the ground only from the sound of his roar.

Hanuman did not need a sword, a bow or even a mace. His own body was his weapon.

So why do we always draw him with a mace?

## 8. Which Weapon Is Stronger Than the Brahmastra?
Source: audio "The Strongest Astra". **(check)** The line "the Brahmastra is the weakest" is the source's claim. The Pashupatastra and the Sudarshana Chakra stories are in the Mahabharata and the Puranas.

**Title:** Which Weapon Is Stronger Than the Brahmastra?

The Brahmastra is the weakest of these three weapons.

First is the Pashupatastra. When Shiva gave it to Arjuna, he warned that using it on a weaker enemy could burn the whole universe. Arjuna did not even need a bow. He could send it with a word, a look, or a thought, and call it back afterward. The Mahabharata places it above the Brahma weapon and the Narayana weapon.

Second is Vishnu's Sudarshana Chakra. Ashwatthama owned the Brahmashira weapon, yet he wanted Krishna's chakra. Krishna let him try to take it. Ashwatthama used all his strength and could not even lift it. The Puranas call the chakra the destroyer of weapons.

Third is Shiva's trident. The Mahabharata compares it with the Pashupatastra itself. In Shiva's hands, it could split the earth, dry up the oceans and destroy the universe.

## 9. Why Did Shiva Kill Yama?
Source: audio "When Mahadev Killed Yamraj".

**Title:** Why Did Shiva Kill Yama, the God of Death?

Shiva once killed Yama himself, for a sixteen-year-old boy.

The boy's name was Markandeya. His father, the sage Mrikandu, had received a boon from Shiva. He would have a wise and good son, but that son would live only sixteen years.

Markandeya grew up as a great devotee of Shiva. On the day he turned sixteen, Yama himself came for his life.

The boy was terrified. He held on to the Shivling and began to pray. Yama threw his rope around Markandeya, and it fell around the Shivling too.

This made Shiva so angry that he came out of the Shivling and killed Yama.

But then nobody was left to govern death. So the gods begged Shiva to bring Yama back. Shiva did. And he blessed Markandeya to stay young forever.

That is why the Brahma Purana calls Shiva the destroyer of death.

## 10. Why Can't We See Sheshnag?
Source: the Hindi audio about Sheshnag. I wrote this one in English; tell me if you want it in Hindi.

**Title:** Why Can't We See Sheshnag?

The whole universe is said to rest on one hood of Sheshnag. And it is as small as a mustard seed.

So where is he? The Bhagavata Purana describes how the worlds were made.

At first, Vishnu slept on the waters. A golden lotus came out of his navel. On top of it, Brahma appeared. Inside the lotus stem, he made fourteen worlds, one below another. At the top is Brahma's own world, and at the bottom is the world of the serpents.

But when the worlds were filled with life, the weight became too great. The lotus stem began to bend and the whole creation started to fall back into the water.

Then the thousand-hooded Ananta Shesha appeared, far below the lowest world. He lifted all fourteen worlds on one hood. Vishnu, as a giant tortoise, holds Shesha up.

And all fourteen worlds sit on that hood like a single mustard seed.

So our earth is smaller than a speck of dust on it. That is why we cannot see him.

## 11. Who Are the Seven Beings Who Cannot Die?
Source: the Chiranjeevi SRT (a long Hindi video). I only used its opening idea: immortality is a gift for some and a burden for others. The list below is the usual traditional list. **(check)** The speaker may use a different one.

**Title:** Who Are the Seven Who Cannot Die?

Seven beings in the old stories are said to live forever. For some, it is a gift. For one, it is a curse.

They are called the Chiranjeevis. Ashwatthama is the first one people think of. He was the son of Drona. He was born with a jewel on his forehead, given by Shiva. After the war he was cursed to wander the earth for thousands of years.

Mahabali is one. He gave everything he owned to Vishnu. Vyasa, who wrote down the Mahabharata, is one. So is Hanuman, who was blessed with long life by Rama. Vibhishana, Ravana's brother, is one. Kripa, the teacher in the Mahabharata, is one. And the last is Parashurama, who still lives on Mount Mahendra.

For some of them, living forever helped the gods' work. For Ashwatthama, it is a punishment that never ends.

Which one would you want to meet? Tell us in the comments.
