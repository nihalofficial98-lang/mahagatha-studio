# Reel 2: Why Do People Go to Kashi? (story 06)

**First-frame title:** Why Do People Go to Kashi?
**Full title (ranked):** 1. Why Do People Go to Kashi? It Started With Shiva and Brahma's Head; 2. Why Do People Go to Varanasi? Shiva, Brahma and a Skull He Could Not Drop; 3. Why Is Kashi Where Sins Are Freed? The Story of Shiva and Brahma's Fifth Head

## Hook
- Context: People travel to Kashi, also called Varanasi, to be freed of their sins. Why?
- Lean: The answer begins with Brahma, the Creator, who once had five heads.
- Snapback: Shiva cut one off in rage, and then it would not let go of him.

## Tagged narration
[curious] Why do people travel to Kashi, also called Varanasi, to be freed of their sins?
[calm] The answer begins with Brahma, the Creator, who once had five heads.
[curious] Shiva cut one off in rage. Then it would not let go of him.
It began with a curse. The sage Narada had said Brahma would desire what must not be desired. When Brahma created his daughter Brahmi, the curse took hold. She saw his gaze and moved away. Each time she moved, a new head grew so he could keep looking at her.
[firm] His sons Marichi and Atri warned him. Brahmi became a cow, a mare, a duck, a doe, a hundred forms in all. She was named Shatarupa.
Shiva appeared in fury. Brahma came to his senses and begged forgiveness. Shiva cut off the fifth head. Brahma's four mouths would recite the Vedas forever, to repent.
[curious] Then Shiva stopped. The severed head was stuck to his left hand.
[calm] Killing a Brahmin was a sin. Atri said every action brings its result, whatever the reason. Brahma had paid, though a curse drove him. So must Shiva.
Shiva wandered the universe, but nothing freed him. The flesh rotted away until only a skull remained. He was called Kapali.
[firm] Then he reached a place on Earth, and the skull fell from his hand. Anyone who came there would be freed of every sin. That place became Kashi, also called Varanasi, and that is why people still go there.

**Length:** 244 words (tags excluded), about 98 s at 0.40 s/word.

## Source lines (story 06)
1. "Brahma once had five heads": fifth head severed "leaving him with only four".
2. Curse: "You shall desire that which must not be desired"; Brahmi is his daughter; heads sprout as she moves away (second to fifth). Told plainly, no detail.
3. Marichi and Atri warn him; Brahmi takes cow, mare, duck, doe forms, one hundred in all; he follows as the male; name Shatarupa.
4. Shiva condemns him, Brahma begs forgiveness, fifth head severed; "your four mouths shall forever keep reciting the Vedas to repent".
5. Head clings to Shiva's left hand: Brahmahatya, "Killing of a Brahmin ... a penalty for his sin."
6. Shatarupa's objection; Atri: "An action will always give birth to a reaction independent of its context"; "The rules of Brahmanda apply equally to all."
7. Wandering, flesh rots, skull remains, name Kapali.
8. Skull falls on Earth; "anyone who came here would be absolved of every sin. The site became Kashi or Varanasi."

## Why it works
Context first, then the cause (Shiva) turned around by the price. Restraint: the curse and the daughter are named once, with no description of desire. Ends resolved with Kashi. Left out: Manu and Shatarupa's closing lament.

## Notes
"Brahmin" is the source's word for the killing, so it is kept; "Brahma's friend" and glosses on Kapali were avoided because the source gives none.
