## Plain narration
Vishnu fought one demon for sixteen thousand years and could not beat him. Then the gods cut off Vishnu's head. So who could kill a demon no god could beat?
Nothing worked on the demon. Vishnu's mace was thrown back and smashed his chariot. His Sudarshana disc stopped at an invisible wall. The demon laughed. Only one who knew his secret could kill him.
Worn out, Vishnu went home to Vaikuntha and fell into a deep sleep, his chin on his bow. The gods, led by Brahma, could not wake him.
Brahma had an idea. Vishnu was sleeping on his bowstring. If it snapped, the sound would wake him. The gods turned into termites and gnawed at the string. Thinner and thinner. Faster and faster. Then it snapped.
The string lashed out and cut off Vishnu's head. Silence. The gods stood frozen. What have we done?
Then Lakshmi, Vishnu's wife, walked in, leaving red footprints. She said the demon's boon was hers. His devotion had touched her. She had blessed him that only a god with a horse's head could kill him.
That was the answer. The gods looked at the headless Vishnu. Lakshmi said the universe is never random. It looks after itself. Vishnu would return as Hayagriva, the Horse-Headed One.
Not long after, Vishnu as Hayagriva, the Horse-Headed One, neighed aloud, and the demon fell silent.
The one god who could kill the unbeatable demon was Vishnu, with the head of a horse.

## Source lines (story 53)
1. Hook: "battling a demon for sixteen thousand years and still not conquered him"; the gods, led by Brahma, "resolved now to conduct a yagna"; the string "severed Vishnu's head". "Preserver" is the source's own epithet for Vishnu.
2. Weapons: Panchajanya conch "failed to set the asura on fire"; Kaumodaki mace "sucked into the demon's body and pitched back at him, pulverising his chariot"; Sudarshana stopped by "an invisible wall". Secret: "Only he who knows my secret can slay me."
3. Sleep: returned to Vaikuntha, "Resting his chin on his Sharanga bow", Yoga Nidra; the devas "could not wake him up".
4. Plan: Brahma, "He's sleeping on his bowstring. If that snaps, its sound will resonate throughout the cosmos"; the gods "changed themselves into termites"; "Thinner and thinner. Faster and faster." then "Twang!".
5. Aftermath: head severed, "Silence", "What have we done?". Lakshmi arrives "leaving red footprints" and says "Forgive me", "This asura is invincible because of a boon", "I was touched by how this demon was doing so much to propitiate me", "only be killed by a god who bears the head of a horse".
6. The demon is unnamed, as in story_53. Hayagriva is only the name of Vishnu's horse-headed avatar ("Sri Hari shall be Hayagriva again").
