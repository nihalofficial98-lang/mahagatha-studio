# Reel 7: Why Did Daksha Curse Narada to Never Find a Home? (story 09)

**First-frame title:** Daksha Cursed Narada
**Full title:** Why Did Daksha Curse Narada to Never Find a Home?
**Alternates:** (a) Narada Spoke to Daksha's Sons. Then They Vanished | (b) Daksha Lost Six Thousand Sons to One Conversation (first-frame: "6,000 Sons Vanished")

## Hook
- Context: Daksha, the son of Brahma, lost six thousand sons.
- Lean: Every single one of them was lost.
- Snapback: The man who took them never raised a hand.

## Tagged narration
[curious] Daksha, the son of Brahma, lost six thousand sons. Every single one. And the man who took them never raised a hand.
[calm] Daksha had five thousand sons, called the Haryashvas. He told them to rule the Earth and have children. They agreed. Before they set out, they gathered where the River Sindhu meets the sea to make offerings.
Narada, the divine sage, was on his way to them. [curious] He asked how they could rule a land they knew nothing about. How would they know if it had too few people or too many? The brothers had no answer. Narada smirked. He told them to learn every detail about the Earth first. They might even find a higher path.
Doubt crept over five thousand faces. The brothers left to explore the Earth. No one saw them again. They never came back.
Daksha and his wife Asikli had another thousand sons, the Shavalashvas. Narada spoke to them in exactly the same way. They vanished too.
[firm] Daksha roared. Who would build homes if everyone became a hermit? Narada said he had only shown them a path. It was their choice to walk it.
Daksha cursed him. Narada had scattered his sons across the Earth, so he would wander the three worlds for ever and never find a home.
[curious] Was Narada a guide, or a thief of sons?

**Length:** 224 words (tags excluded), about 90 s at 0.40 s/word. Trimmed per review: hook rewritten, size question and Sindhu simile cut.

## Source lines (story 09)
1. Hook: "five thousand sons ... Haryashvas" plus "another thousand sons ... Shavalashvas". The source never says "six thousand"; it is the sum of the two batches. "Brahma's son, Daksha" (line 9).
2. "instructed all of them to become rulers of the Earth and continue procreating"; "readily agreed"; "congregated at a point where River Sindhu met the sea to offer oblations".
3. "someone was headed their way" ... Narada, "Devarshi". Questions: history or geography, circumference, under- or over-populating. "The brothers had no answer. The Devarshi smirked." "seek out every detail about Earth"; "a different path. A higher path."
4. "five thousand faces were now creasing with doubt"; "no one saw them again ... they never came back." (the Sindhu simile and the size question are cut for length)
5. "Daksha and Asikli gave birth to another thousand sons and named them Shavalashvas"; Narada "in exactly the same manner"; "vanished".
6. "'Who will build homes, Narada, if everyone becomes a hermit?'"; "I only showed them a path ... their choice to walk upon it."
7. Curse: "scattered my sons throughout Earth"; "wander through these three realms and never find an abode."
Cut: Manu/Prasuti intro and the whole genealogy (sixty daughters, Prajapati, Sati). Closing question is editorial, not in the source.

## Why it works
Daksha named in sentence 1, stakes in a plain count, and a snapback that the thief used only words. Complete for a stranger: command, trap of questions, disappearance, repeat, protest, curse. Mostly indirect speech, names re-introduced, ends on one open question that splits comments. Not a series pointer.
