# Reel 5: How Was Shiva Born From Brahma's Anger? (story 03)

**First-frame title:** Shiva Born From Anger
**Full title:** How Was Shiva Born From Brahma's Anger?
**Alternates:** (a) Shiva Was Born From Brahma's Anger. His First Demand Was a Name; (b) Brahma's Four Sons Said No. Then Shiva Appeared

## Hook
- Context: Shiva was born from another god's anger.
- Lean: Brahma had just been refused by his own sons.
- Snapback: The newborn's first demand was not power. It was a name.

## Tagged narration
[curious] Shiva was born from another god's anger. His first demand was not for power. It was for a name.
[calm] Brahma, the Creator, had made four sons from his mind. They were child sages called the Kumaras. He told them to help him fill the world with living beings. They folded their hands and said no.
They said they were grateful, but they did not want to be bound by life and death. They wanted to meditate on the infinite. They asked only that they never grow old. Brahma argued, then gave in. As his sons left, rage rose in him.
[curious] A fiery beam burst from his forehead. A being stepped out of it with blazing red eyes. He roared, "What is my name?"
[calm] Brahma sat in awe. The being roared again. "Tell me my name." Brahma remembered that the root word for both red and roaring is Rud. He said, "Rudra. I name you Rudra."
This was Shiva, also called Mahadeva. Brahma created, Vishnu preserved, and Shiva was the Destroyer.
Brahma asked him to help with creation. Shiva agreed and began making beings. But he made copies of himself, and each was immortal. Eleven Rudras stood before Brahma.
[firm] Brahma said creation needed mortals, who die and are born again. Shiva said he could only make immortals. If that spoiled the plan, he would rather meditate. Then he left.
[curious] The god born from Brahma's anger could only make what never dies.

**Length:** 240 words (tags excluded), about 96 s at 0.40 s/word.

## Source lines (story 03)
1. Hook: "What is my name?" / "Rudra. I name you Rudra."
2. Kumaras: "from his mind issued forth Kumaras, or four child sages"; task to "Populate Brahmanda through the act of procreation"; "The Kumaras folded their hands and said, 'No!'"
3. Refusal: "Do you dare refuse to do your father's bidding?"; "we do not wish to remain confined within the circle of life and death"; boon "that we may never age".
4. "Brahma argued some more, but finally yielded ... intense rage surging inside him."
5. "it sparked from his forehead in the form of a fiery beam ... eyes blazing red ... 'What is my name?'"
6. Naming: "the root word for both red and roaring is 'Rud'"; "Rudra. I name you Rudra."
7. "Rudra was Shiva. Mahadeva ... completed the cosmic trinity"; Creator, Preserver, Destroyer.
8. Shiva agrees to help and "started generating one being after another"; "Eleven Rudras", "all immortal".
9. "Creation must give rise to mortals"; "I can only generate immortals ... I would rather meditate."
10. Eleven sites allotted; "Mahadeva, whose hallowed task was to destroy, cannot create." Closing line paraphrases this.

## Why it works
Context and the odd snapback (a name, not power) are in line 1; the cause (sons said no, then rage) is told in order. Ends on a short resolved line. The eleven sites (heart, senses, elements) and the later "fourth/final trouble" are left out. Rudra is named exactly as the source tells it: the being demands it, Brahma gives it.
