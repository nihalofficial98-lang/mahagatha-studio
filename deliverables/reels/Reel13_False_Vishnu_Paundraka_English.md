## Plain narration
King Paundraka dressed up as Vishnu to replace Krishna. He painted himself blue and strapped on wooden arms. Why would a king make himself look so foolish?
Paundraka ruled the eastern land of Pundra. He hated Krishna, the Yadava king of Dwaravati. When Krishna killed his friend Jarasandh, Paundraka raged. His flatterers told him Krishna was a thief, and that Paundraka was the true Vishnu.
They said it again and again. Paundraka believed it. He renamed himself Vasudeva, a name of Vishnu, and sent Krishna a decree. Give up the title and the divine weapons, or fight.
Krishna smiled, then laughed. He said he would fight.
Paundraka marched on Dwaravati. His body was blue and his cloth was yellow. Two wooden arms were strapped behind his own to look four-armed. A Garuda banner flew over his chariot.
Krishna's soldiers laughed. Krishna said gods create humans, but a human can never be a god. Paundraka was only a man behind a mask.
Paundraka snapped. He called Krishna the fraud and demanded his weapons. Krishna said, "Take them. Take them all." He hurled his conch, bow, mace and disc.
The disc cut off Paundraka's head. His wooden arms fell off. The rain washed the blue paint off his skin.
Krishna warned everyone to beware of the Paundrakas of the Earth. One false god, he said, would plague people more than a hundred demons. A lie told again and again had made a king believe he was a god.
