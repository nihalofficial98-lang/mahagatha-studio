## Plain narration
King Paundraka dressed up as Vishnu to replace Krishna. His courtiers swore it worked. Krishna's army had a different reaction.
Paundraka ruled the eastern land of Pundra. He was a friend of Jarasandh and an enemy of Krishna, the Yadava king of Dwaravati. When Krishna killed Jarasandh, Paundraka raged. His flatterers told him Krishna was a thief, and that Paundraka was the true Vishnu.
They said it again and again. Paundraka believed it. He renamed himself Vasudeva, a name of Vishnu, and sent Krishna a decree. Give up the title and the divine weapons, or fight.
Krishna smiled, then laughed. He said he would fight.
Paundraka marched on Dwaravati. He had painted his body blue and wore yellow cloth. He had strapped two wooden arms behind his own to look four-armed. He wore a fake Kaustubha gem, and a Garuda banner flew over his chariot.
Krishna's soldiers laughed. Some rolled on the ground. Krishna said gods create humans, but a human can never be a god. Paundraka was only a man behind a mask.
Paundraka snapped. He called Krishna the fraud and demanded his weapons. Krishna said, "Take them. Take them all." He hurled his conch, bow, mace and disc.
The disc cut off Paundraka's head. His wooden arms fell off. The rain washed the blue paint off his skin.
Krishna warned everyone to beware of the Paundrakas of the Earth. One false god, he said, would plague people more than a hundred demons.
