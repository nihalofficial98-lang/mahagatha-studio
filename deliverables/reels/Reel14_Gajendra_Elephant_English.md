## Plain narration
A crocodile clamped onto the leg of Gajendra, king of the elephants. His whole herd walked away. When Vishnu came, his first words were a name Gajendra did not expect.
Near the Ocean of Milk stood Mount Trikuta. Beside it was a lotus pond where elephants came to drink. Their leader was Gajendra, a king tusker devoted to Vishnu.
One day, Gajendra trumpeted in terror. A crocodile had seized his leg. Blood clouded the pond. The herd knew a crocodile is stronger in water. They watched helplessly, and then they left.
Gajendra was alone. He stopped fighting. He lifted a white lotus in his trunk and prayed to Vishnu. He said he could do nothing to save himself. Protect me, he said, or free me if my hour has come.
The morning turned dark. A blue shape spread across the sky, a blue finger holding the Sudarshana disc. The disc flew down and cut off the crocodile's head.
Vishnu smiled. He asked, "Have you learnt your lesson, Indradyumna?"
Gajendra froze. The name stirred a past life. He had been a king named Indradyumna. When the sage Agastya came to his court, he did not even open his eyes to greet him. The sage cursed him to be born as an elephant, to learn to surrender.
The crocodile, Vishnu said, was a gandharva named Huhu, who had insulted the sage Deval.
Gajendra bowed to the ground before Vishnu.

2. Attack: "A graha, or crocodile, had seized one of his legs"; "teeth were digging deep ... Blood was gushing"; the herd "departing. Leaving him to his fate."
3. Prayer: "There was only one thing to be done"; "Holding aloft a white lotus in his trunk"; "Protect me if you wish that I should remain alive. Or liberate me if my hour of death is here."
