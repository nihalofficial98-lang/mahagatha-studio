## Plain narration
King Harishchandra gave away his kingdom and sold his wife and himself. Then his son died, and he still would not break his word. Why would he not break his word, even for his dead son?
Harishchandra, king of Ayodhya, had once dodged a vow to the water god Varuna to keep his son alive. He swore never again. A spirit made him insult the sage Vishwamitra. He promised the sage anything, and gave him his throne, wealth and Ayodhya.
The sage demanded a fee, dakshina. In Kashi, they found no work. His wife, Taramati, insisted that he sell her as a maid. He refused until she threatened to die. It was only half.
Vishwamitra ordered him to sell himself to a Chandala, a man who burned the dead. He worked at the cremation ground.
One night, a woman came with her dead son and no money for the fee. She was Taramati. The boy was Rohita, bitten by a snake.
She begged him to break his word just once. He refused to cremate his son without the fee. He had given his word to his master. She grabbed an axe to end her life.
Then Vishwamitra called her name. Rohita ran to her alive. The sage said he could not defeat the king, and gave back everything. The Chandala was Yama.
That is why he would not break his word, even for his son. He had broken it once, was still paying for it, and had sworn never again.
