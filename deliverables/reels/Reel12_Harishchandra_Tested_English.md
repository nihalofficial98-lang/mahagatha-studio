## Plain narration
King Harishchandra lost his kingdom, his wife and his son. He lost them to keep one promise. And the hardest test came at a cremation ground.
Harishchandra, king of Ayodhya, had once broken a vow to the water god Varuna. He swore never again. One day a spirit made him insult the sage Vishwamitra. To make amends, he promised the sage anything. Vishwamitra took his throne, his wealth and Ayodhya.
The king left with his wife, Taramati, and son, Rohita. The sage demanded dakshina, a payment for accepting charity. The family found no work in Kashi. Taramati asked to be sold as a maid. Harishchandra refused until she threatened to die. It raised only half the payment.
Vishwamitra told him to sell himself to a Chandala, a man who burned the dead. Harishchandra agreed. He worked at the cremation ground.
One night, a woman came with her dead son and no money for the fee. Harishchandra said he could not help. Then he saw her face. She was Taramati. The boy was Rohita, bitten by a snake.
She begged him to break his word just once. He refused. He had given his word to his master, and the gods were watching. She grabbed an axe to end her life.
Then Vishwamitra called her name. Rohita ran to her alive. The sage said he could not defeat the king, and gave back everything. The Chandala was Yama, the god of death. He said the king's name would stand for truth.
