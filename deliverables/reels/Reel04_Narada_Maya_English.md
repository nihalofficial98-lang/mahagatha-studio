# Reel 4: Narada Asked Vishnu What Maya Is. Then This Happened (story 07)

**First-frame title:** Narada's Maya Test
**Full title:** Narada Asked Vishnu What Maya Is. Then This Happened
**Alternates:** (a) Narada Went to Fetch Water for Vishnu and Lost Everything; (b) A Sage Asked Vishnu About Maya. Why Was Vishnu Still Thirsty?

## Hook
- Context: Narada, a great sage, asked Vishnu, the Preserver, what maya is.
- Lean: Vishnu did not explain. He asked for a glass of water.
- Snapback: Narada never brought it, and that was the answer.

## Tagged narration
[curious] Narada, a great sage, asked Vishnu, the Preserver, what maya is. Vishnu did not explain. He asked for water. Narada never brought it, and that was the answer.
[calm] Narada had come to Vaikuntha, Vishnu's home, to tell him how his curse had come true on his father, Brahma. Vishnu smiled and said it was all maya. Narada asked what that meant.
Vishnu said he was thirsty. He told Narada to fetch some water, and then he would answer.
Narada walked to the river outside. By the bank he saw a young woman. She was so lovely that he forgot Vishnu, his errand and his thirst.
[curious] He married her right there and built a house on the sand by the river.
[calm] Years passed. Narada became a father, then a grandfather. The house had become his whole world.
Then one day the sky turned grey. Thunder roared. Rain poured, and the river flooded. The water swept his family away.
[firm] Suddenly Narada was back in Vaikuntha, standing before Vishnu. Vishnu asked, "Where is the water? I told you I am thirsty."
Narada stood stunned. His wife, his children, his home. None of it had been real.
Vishnu said that was maya. Attachment to a world that does not last. It makes us chase what slips away like sand.
[curious] Then he smiled and warned Narada that there would be much more maya to come.

**Length:** 230 words (tags excluded), about 92 s at 0.40 s/word.

## Source lines (story 07)
1. Hook: "What is maya?" / "I feel thirsty. Get me some water and I shall tell you."
2. Setting: Narada comes to Vaikuntha after "Four heads! My father has four heads now." (curse as shown in the previous scene; Brahma is his father in the text); "It's all maya."
3. Thirst request: "Get me some water and I shall tell you."
4. River maiden: "forgot Vishnu and his thirst. Forgot everything else."
5. "He married her then and there and made a house on those sands".
6. "Years came and years went ... father and a grandfather"; household "had now become his universe".
7. Flood: sky turns grey, thunder, rain, "swept Narada's family away".
8. "suddenly found himself back in Vaikuntha"; "Where's the water, Narada?"
9. "My wife? My children? My home? ... It had all been one giant illusion."
10. "This suffering comes from maya ... All else is sand that slips through the fingers."
11. "Prepare yourself, Narada. There's going to be much maya."

## Why it works
Context in line 1, a simple odd request as the lean, and the snapback says the missing water is the answer. The whole illusion plays out in order, the twist is the cut back to Vaikuntha, and it ends on Vishnu's warning. Left out: Shatarupa, Parabrahman and moksha, which need earlier stories. Note: the source never says the curse is Narada's by name in this scene beyond "his curse", and the four-heads line is kept to one clause.
