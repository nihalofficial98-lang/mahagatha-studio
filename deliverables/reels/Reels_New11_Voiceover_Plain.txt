REEL N1 (3 Dec): Why Didn't Parashurama Kill Ravana?
Parashurama killed the king who once captured Ravana. Then he walked away and never fought Ravana himself.
Ravana had a boon that protected him from gods and demons. He never asked for safety from humans, because he thought humans were beneath him. Parashurama was a human. So the boon was not the reason.
He was not too weak, either. And no verse says he spared Ravana because Ravana was born a Brahmin.
The real reason is simpler. Parashurama had finished his work. He had given the whole earth to the sage Kashyapa and gone to live on Mount Mahendra.
Then, years later, he blocked the road in front of young Rama. Rama had just broken Shiva's great bow. Parashurama held out the bow of Vishnu and told him to string it. Rama did. Then Rama aimed the arrow at the worlds Parashurama had earned by his penance, and the worlds were gone. Parashurama said there was no shame in losing to Vishnu himself.
The gods had already asked Vishnu to be born as Dasharatha's son to kill Ravana. The job had Rama's name on it from the start.

REEL N2 (4 Dec): Why Did Shiva Kill Yama?
Shiva once killed Yama, the god of death himself. He did it for a boy who was only sixteen.
The boy was Markandeya. His father, the sage Mrikandu, had asked Shiva for a son. Shiva gave him a choice. A foolish son who would live long, or a wise son who would live only sixteen years. Mrikandu chose the wise son.
Markandeya grew up to be a great devotee of Shiva. On the day he turned sixteen, Yama came for his life. The boy held on to the Shivling and prayed. Yama threw his rope around the boy, and it fell around the Shivling too.
This made Shiva so angry that he came out of the Shivling and killed Yama.
But then no one was left to govern death. The gods begged Shiva to bring Yama back. Shiva did, and he blessed Markandeya to stay sixteen forever.
That is why the Brahma Purana calls Shiva the destroyer of death. Would you have saved Yama? Tell us in the comments.

REEL N3 (5 Dec): Is Ashwatthama Still Alive?
Every morning, fresh flowers are already lying on a Shivling in a temple in Madhya Pradesh, before anyone arrives. Local people say Ashwatthama puts them there, the Mahabharata warrior who was cursed never to die.
The temple is the Gupteshwar Mahadev Temple, inside the Asirgarh Fort. Locals say the man is Ashwatthama, the warrior who lived through the Kurukshetra war.
There is a second story from the banks of the Narmada river. Travelers say a very tall stranger comes to them. He has a wound on his forehead that never heals. He asks for oil or turmeric to ease the pain.
People say he has been wandering for thousands of years since the war. In these stories, he does not ask for money or power. He only asks for relief from his pain.
But these are stories that people pass down. What do the old books really say? Did Krishna truly curse him to live forever?

REEL N4 (6 Dec): Hanuman vs Kumbhakarna
Hanuman threw mountains at Kumbhakarna. Kumbhakarna drove a spear into his chest, and Hanuman threw up blood.
It happened in the war in Lanka. The giant took every mountain peak and did not fall. Then he struck back, and the Valmiki Ramayana says Hanuman was badly wounded, with blood pouring from his mouth. But he was not defeated.
When Kumbhakarna hurled his spear at Sugriva, Hanuman jumped in front of it, caught it and broke it across his knee.
Later, Kumbhakarna grabbed Sugriva. Hanuman thought about growing as large as a mountain and crushing the giant with his fists. But he trusted Sugriva to escape on his own. And Sugriva did.
Kumbhakarna kept fighting until he faced Rama. Rama cut off his arms, then his legs, and in the end cut off his head with one divine arrow.

REEL N5 (6 Dec): When Will Vishnu's Last Avatar Come?
The Vishnu Purana names the village where Vishnu's last avatar will be born. It even names his father.
The village is Shambhala. His father is a Brahmin named Vishnuyasha. The child will be Kalki.
The same book describes the age we live in, the Kali Yuga. A person will be respected for money, not for goodness. People will do religion only to show others. Relatives will stay together only for gain. And people will win in court with lies and cheat their own family for money. Which of these is not true today?
But the Purana also says what comes at the end. When evil goes beyond every limit, Vishnu will be born as Kalki, with divine power. People's minds will become clear like crystal, as if they have woken after a long night.
And the last part is the kindest. In earlier ages, people needed years of meditation, or great fires, or long worship. In the Kali Yuga, saying God's name is enough.

REEL N6 (7 Dec): Did Hanuman Ever Use a Mace?
Hanuman, the god we always draw with a huge mace, never fought with a mace in the Valmiki Ramayana.
In the Sundara Kanda, Ravana sent thousands of demons to kill him. Hanuman tore a heavy iron bar out of a gateway and used it to destroy them.
When Ravana sent the seven sons of his ministers, Hanuman fought them with his bare hands. He struck some with his palms and some with his feet.
So in battle he used his fists, his nails, his chest and his legs. Some enemies fell to the ground only from the sound of his roar.
Hanuman did not need a sword, a bow or even a mace. His own body was his weapon.
So why do we always draw him with a mace? Do you know the answer? Tell us in the comments.

REEL N7 (8 Dec): Which Weapon Is Stronger Than the Brahmastra?
The Brahmastra is the weapon that stops entire armies. And it is the weakest of these three.
First, the Pashupatastra. When Shiva gave it to Arjuna, he warned that using it on a weaker enemy could burn the whole universe. Arjuna did not even need a bow. He could send it with a word, a look or a thought, and call it back. The Mahabharata places it above the Brahma weapon.
Second, Vishnu's Sudarshana Chakra. Ashwatthama owned the Brahmashira weapon, yet he wanted Krishna's chakra. Krishna let him try. Ashwatthama used all his strength and could not even lift it.
Third, Shiva's trident. The Mahabharata compares it with the Pashupatastra itself. In Shiva's hands, it could split the earth, dry up the oceans and destroy the universe.

REEL N8 (9 Dec): Why Can't We See Sheshnag?
The whole universe rests on one hood of Sheshnag. And it is as small as a mustard seed.
So where is he? The Bhagavata Purana tells how the worlds were made. At first, Vishnu slept on the ocean. A golden lotus came out of his navel, and Brahma appeared on it.
Inside the lotus stem, Brahma made fourteen worlds, one below another. The top world is his own, and the bottom world belongs to the serpents.
But when the worlds filled with life, the weight became too great. The stem began to bend, and the whole creation started to fall back into the water.
Then the thousand-hooded Ananta Shesha appeared, far below the lowest world. He lifted all fourteen worlds on a single hood, and Vishnu, as a giant tortoise, holds him up.
All fourteen worlds sit on his hood like one mustard seed. That means our earth is smaller than a speck of dust on it. That is why we cannot see him.

REEL N9 (10 Dec): Did Indra Almost Defeat Ravana?
Ravana beat kings and gods. But Indra once trapped him, and only his own son could save him.
Ravana had decided to attack heaven. He wanted Indra's kingdom. At first, both armies fought hard.
Then Indra decided to take Ravana alive. His army surrounded the demon king, and for the first time Ravana was struggling only to escape.
At that moment, Ravana's son Meghanada entered the battle. He knew he could not beat Indra in an open fight. So he used his magic to become invisible and attacked from places Indra could not see.
In the end, he overpowered Indra and took him to Lanka as a prisoner. That is why Meghanada is called Indrajit, the one who won over Indra.
The matter became so serious that Brahma himself came to Lanka to ask for Indra's release.

REEL N10 (10 Dec): Who Are the Seven Who Cannot Die?
Hanuman, Parashurama, Ashwatthama and four others in Hindu stories can never die. For six of them, it is a gift. For one of them, it is a curse.
They are called the Chiranjeevis. The first is Ashwatthama, the son of Drona. He was born with a jewel on his forehead, given by Shiva. After the Kurukshetra war, he was cursed to wander the earth for thousands of years.
Mahabali is the second. He gave everything he owned to Vishnu. Vyasa, who wrote down the Mahabharata, is the third. Hanuman, who was blessed with long life by Rama, is the fourth.
Vibhishana, Ravana's brother, is the fifth. Kripa, the teacher in the Mahabharata, is the sixth. And the seventh is Parashurama, who still lives on Mount Mahendra.
For most of them, living forever helped the gods' work. For Ashwatthama, it is a punishment that never ends.

REEL N11 (11 Dec): Did Ravana Really Never Touch Sita?
Ravana did not hurt Sita. And it was not because he was a good man.
People say this proves Ravana was great. The old books say something different.
When Hanuman entered Ravana's palace, he saw rooms filled with women. The Ramayana says Ravana had taken them during his wars.
Ravana had also attacked an apsara named Punjikasthala. When Brahma learned of it, he cursed Ravana. If Ravana ever forced himself on a woman again, his head would split into a hundred pieces.
When Ravana carried Sita away, he took her by force. And in the Yuddha Kanda, he himself admits why he never forced her into his bed. It was not respect. He feared the curse.
