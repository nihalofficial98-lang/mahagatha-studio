## Plain narration
Out of every creature in the universe, why did Vishnu choose Garuda to carry him? Not because Garuda was the strongest. It was because of what he did with the Amrut, and who he did it for.
Garuda, half bird and half man, was the son of Vinata. The snakes kept her as a slave. They would free her for Amrut, the drink of immortality. The gods guarded it with fire, spinning blades and two snakes. Garuda broke through all of it.
On the way back, he froze. Vishnu stood in his path. He knew why Garuda took the Amrut. He was happy that Garuda had not tasted any.
Vishnu offered him a gift. Garuda asked for two things. He wanted to stay above Vishnu. And he wanted to live forever and never be sick, without drinking the Amrut. Vishnu said yes to both. In return, he asked Garuda to carry him. A carrier like that is called a vahana.
Further on, Indra, king of the gods, threw his thunder weapon, the Vajra, at Garuda. It took only one golden feather from him. Indra saw his strength and stepped aside.
Garuda put the pot down before the snakes. They freed his mother.
Later, Garuda said he would carry Vishnu. Vishnu had made him his mount.
That is why Garuda became Vishnu's vahana. He won the Amrut for his mother's freedom and never tasted it. Vishnu rewarded him, and Garuda gave his strength in return.
