[mysterious] Kacha wanted to be killed. Burned. Ground to ash. And swallowed by his enemy's guru.
It was the only way to steal a secret that could bring the dead back to life.
[darkly] It worked. But what he did to the girl who loved him was worse than death.

Every time the gods killed a demon, one old man brought him back to life. Shukracharya, guru of the demons. His secret was Mritasanjivani, the power to revive the dead.
As long as he held it, the gods could never win.

Brihaspati, guru of the gods, called a council in heaven. His face was grim. He spoke one word. [slowly] "Mritasanjivani."
[surprised] "You want us to steal it?" said Indra, king of the gods. "From Shukracharya? He will never share it."
[calmly] "Not with force," said Brihaspati. "I have someone he will never suspect. And I have a plan."

A few days later, a young man walked into Shukracharya's ashram and asked to be his student. The guru tied a sacred thread on his wrist, a promise to keep him safe. He did not know whose son the boy was.
"I am Kacha," said the young man. His new teacher's daughter was watching him from the doorway.
[softly] "I am Devayani," she said, and lowered her eyes.

Soon she was in love with him. Every morning he sang hymns by her window. Every evening he brought her wild flowers from the woods.
And every night, a shadow slipped into his room. Brihaspati.
[whispers] "Do you have it, Kacha?"
[whispers] "Not yet, father. But his daughter loves me more every day."

Then one evening, Kacha saw the demons brewing wine for their guru. They burned a deer and stirred its ashes into the cup. Shukracharya drank it all.
Kacha stared at the cup. [slowly] A terrible idea came to him. What if those ashes were his?
It could kill him. But it was the only way. The demons hated Brihaspati's son. Shukracharya loved his wine. And Devayani loved Kacha exactly as much as she loved her father. Not more. Not less.

The plan had three stages. Kacha would control two. The third was in Devayani's hands.
[coldly] He would not tell her. It was better if she did not know.

Stage one. A wild feast of the demons. Kacha picked up a cup, pretended to be drunk, and whispered to a demon, [whispers] "Shall I tell you a secret? I am the son of Brihaspati."
The demon choked on his drink. He told another. And another. Soon the demons were angry. They crowded around Kacha. One reached for his throat.
[shouting] "Stop!" shouted Shukracharya. "The thread on his wrist binds me to protect him. He is my student. Treat him as one of your own."
The demons backed away.

That night, Devayani stormed into his room, shaking with anger. Kacha soothed her. She left, calm again.
He lay awake. The demons wanted him dead. [coldly] Exactly as he planned.

Stage two. At dawn, Kacha took the guru's horses to graze. Deep in the forest, he stopped. And waited. He was waiting for the demons. They were angry at him, and he knew they would come.
[whispers] A twig cracked. "The demons are coming."

He piled up branches and lit a fire. The demons' footsteps grew louder. Closer. Closer. He never looked up. He only watched their feet. He did not run. He did not fight. The demons had knives and claws.
They stood all around him.
His body shook, but his mind was calm. Everything was going as he had planned. [slowly] He was going to die, but he would not stay dead.

[darkly] The demons killed him. They burned his body and ground the ashes to powder. They stirred the powder into Shukracharya's wine, the way they had done with the deer.
Shukracharya drank the whole cup. [slowly] He did not know that he had just swallowed Kacha.

That night, Devayani stumbled out of the forest, holding a piece of burnt cloth. She stood before her father, Shukracharya.
[crying] "Father," she said, "this is all that is left of Kacha. Your demons killed him. They hacked him and burned him."
[angry] "How dare they! I will punish every one of them..."
He stopped. Devayani was laughing. Tears poured down her face, and she was laughing.
[laughing through tears] "Punish them? You, father? You drank him. You drank my Kacha."
She wiped a black speck from his lips. [coldly] "This is his ash. The demons put it in your wine."

Shukracharya closed his eyes in horror. Then he spoke. [sadly] "From today, any Brahmin who drinks wine will lose his wisdom and his virtue." He had failed to keep Kacha safe.
"You know what you have to do," said Devayani.
[heavily] "Mritasanjivani. But he is inside me. To rise again, Kacha must tear my body apart. And I am willing."
She froze. She had begged for a life, and not seen the price. Kacha's life demanded another life. Her father's.

[pleading] "Never!" cried Devayani. "I cannot live without you. I love you both. Not more. Not less."
"Then you must choose. Him or me?"
[whispers] Her eyes lit up. "Not him or you. Both. Give me the mantra. When Kacha comes out of you, I will bring you back."
Shukracharya was afraid. He had prayed for a thousand years to earn this secret. And his daughter asked, [softly] "Don't you trust me, father?"
He looked at his daughter for a long time. Then he nodded. He wrote it down. [firmly] "Use it once. Then destroy it. Promise me."
"I promise."

[darkly] He closed his eyes and began to chant. His belly shook. Then his chest, his face, his arms.
A finger came out of his eye. A foot came out of his belly.

Kacha came out of Shukracharya's body, covered in blood.
[crying] "Kacha!" Devayani held him, laughing and crying. She did not see his eyes.
[slowly] He smiled, but not the way he used to.

"Now I must bring father back."
"Let me do it," said Kacha.
She hid the mantra behind her. "No. It is father's secret. Only I may use it."
[softly] "Only you? Am I anyone? I am you. You are me. The guru gave me back my life. Let me give his back."
His eyes held hers. Her fingers loosened. The mantra fell.
[slowly] Kacha caught it.

He read it over the guru's body. The secret the demons were proud of. A gift from Shiva. He read every word and learned it by heart.
Shukracharya stood up. He took a step toward his daughter. Then he saw the mantra in Kacha's hand. He saw Kacha smiling.
[sadly] He knew. There was no rage on his face. Only pain. And he fell to the ground.

Kacha began to walk away.
[desperately] "Kacha!" Devayani ran after him. "I told father about us. Speak to him so we can marry."
[coldly] "You still don't understand?" He did not even look at her.
Then she understood. [whispers] "Mritasanjivani?" she whispered.
[coldly] "I came for what my father sent me for."

She touched her father's blood on her cheek and stared at her fingers.
[crying] "You made me betray my father so you could keep your promise to yours! How could you be so cruel?"
[coldly] "If I was cruel, the universe would have stopped me. I only found a friend. I never made any promises. And now that I was born from your father's body, I am his son too. That makes you my sister."

She watched him go. Her father lay on the ground, and the man she loved was leaving. She was crying. Then she spoke.
[angry] "I curse you, Kacha. The secret you stole will never work for you."
Kacha turned. [coldly] "Then I will teach it to others. It will work for them. And I curse you, Devayani. You will never find the love you seek."
