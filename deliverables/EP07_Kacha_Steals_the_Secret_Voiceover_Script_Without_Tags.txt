Kacha wanted to be killed. Burned. Ground to ash. And swallowed by his enemy's guru.
It was the only way to steal a secret that could bring the dead back to life.
It worked. But what he did to the girl who loved him was worse than death.

Every time the gods killed a demon, one old man brought him back to life. His name was Shukracharya, guru of the demons. His secret was Mritasanjivani, the power to revive the dead.
As long as he held it, the gods could never win.

Brihaspati, guru of the gods, called a council in heaven. His face was grim. He spoke one word. Mritasanjivani.
Indra, king of the gods, asked if they really had to steal it from Shukracharya. The old guru would never share it.
Brihaspati said they would not use force. He had someone Shukracharya would never suspect. And he had a plan.

A few days later, a young man walked into Shukracharya's ashram and asked to be his student. The guru tied a sacred thread on his wrist, a promise to keep him safe. He did not know whose son the boy was.
The young man told him his name was Kacha. Shukracharya's daughter was watching him from the doorway. She told him her name was Devayani, and she lowered her eyes.

Soon she was in love with him. Every morning he sang hymns by her window. Every evening he brought her wild flowers from the woods.
And every night, a shadow slipped into his room. It was Brihaspati. He asked Kacha if he had the secret yet.
Kacha said not yet, but the guru's daughter loved him more every day.

Then one evening, Kacha saw the demons brewing wine for their guru. They burned a deer and stirred its ashes into the cup. Shukracharya drank it all.
Kacha stared at the cup. A terrible idea came to him. What if those ashes were his?
It could kill him. But it was the only way. The demons hated Brihaspati's son. Shukracharya loved his wine. And Devayani loved Kacha exactly as much as she loved her father. Not more. Not less.

The plan had three stages. Kacha would control two. The third was in Devayani's hands.
He would not tell her. It was better if she did not know.

Stage one. The demons held a wild feast. Kacha picked up a cup, pretended to be drunk, and whispered to one demon that he was the son of Brihaspati.
The demon choked on his drink. He told another. And another. Soon the demons were angry. They crowded around Kacha. One reached for his throat.
Then Shukracharya shouted for them to stop. He said the thread on Kacha's wrist bound him to protect his student, and he ordered the demons to treat Kacha as one of their own.
The demons backed away.

That night, Devayani stormed into his room, shaking with anger. Kacha soothed her. She left, calm again.
He lay awake. The demons wanted him dead. Exactly as he planned.

Stage two. At dawn, Kacha took the guru's horses to graze. Deep in the forest, he stopped. And waited. He was waiting for the demons. They were angry at him, and he knew they would come.
Then a twig cracked. The demons were coming.

He piled up branches and lit a fire. The demons' footsteps grew louder. Closer. Closer. He never looked up. He only watched their feet. He did not run. He did not fight. The demons had knives and claws.
They stood all around him.
His body shook, but his mind was calm. Everything was going as he had planned. He was going to die, but he would not stay dead.

The demons killed him. They burned his body and ground the ashes to powder. They stirred the powder into Shukracharya's wine, the way they had done with the deer.
Shukracharya drank the whole cup. He did not know that he had just swallowed Kacha.

That night, Devayani stumbled out of the forest, holding a piece of burnt cloth. She stood before her father, Shukracharya. She told him it was all that was left of Kacha. His demons had killed him.
Shukracharya said he would punish every one of them. Then he stopped. Devayani was laughing. Tears poured down her face, and she was laughing.
She asked how he could punish them, when he had drunk Kacha himself. She wiped a black speck from his lips. It was Kacha's ash. The demons had put it in his wine.

Shukracharya closed his eyes in horror. Then he spoke. From that day, he said, any Brahmin who drinks wine will lose his wisdom and his virtue. He had failed to keep Kacha safe.
Devayani told him he knew what he had to do.
He did. He had to use Mritasanjivani. But Kacha was inside him. To rise again, Kacha would have to tear his body apart. Shukracharya said he was willing.
Devayani froze. She had begged for a life, and not seen the price. Kacha's life demanded another life. Her father's.

She cried out that she could not live without her father. She loved them both. Not more. Not less.
Shukracharya said she must choose. Kacha or him.
Her eyes lit up. She said she did not have to choose. She asked him for the mantra. When Kacha came out of him, she would bring him back.
He had prayed for a thousand years to earn this secret. And his daughter asked if he did not trust her. Then he nodded. He wrote the mantra down. He told her to use it once, and then destroy it. She promised.

He closed his eyes and began to chant. His belly shook. Then his chest, his face, his arms.
A finger came out of his eye. A foot came out of his belly.

Kacha came out of Shukracharya's body, covered in blood.
Devayani held him, laughing and crying. She did not see his eyes.
He smiled, but not the way he used to.

Devayani said she must bring her father back now.
Kacha asked to do it himself.
She hid the mantra behind her. She said it was her father's secret, and only she could use it.
Kacha asked if he was anyone else. He said he was her, and she was him. The guru had given him back his life, and now he wanted to give the guru's life back.
His eyes held hers. Her fingers loosened. The mantra fell.
Kacha caught it.

He read it over the guru's body. The secret the demons were proud of. A gift from Shiva. He read every word and learned it by heart.
Shukracharya stood up. He took a step toward his daughter. Then he saw the mantra in Kacha's hand. He saw Kacha smiling.
He knew. There was no rage on his face. Only pain. And he fell to the ground.

Kacha began to walk away.
Devayani ran after him. She said she had told her father about them, and asked him to speak to the guru so they could marry.
Kacha asked if she still did not understand. He did not even look at her.
Then she understood. Mritasanjivani. He told her he had come for what his father had sent him for.

She touched her father's blood on her cheek and stared at her fingers. She asked how he could be so cruel, to make her betray her father so he could keep his promise to his own.
Kacha said that if he were cruel, the universe would have stopped him. He had only found a friend, and he had never made any promises. And now that he was born from her father's body, he was her father's son too. That made her his sister.

She watched him go. Her father lay on the ground, and the man she loved was leaving. She was crying. Then she spoke.
She cursed him. The secret he had stolen, she said, would never work for him.
Kacha turned. He said he would teach it to others, and it would work for them. And he cursed her back. She would never find the love she sought.
