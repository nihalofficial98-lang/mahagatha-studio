# Triple hook method

Viewers only keep watching content they have a vested interest in. A hook has three steps, in this order.

## 1. Context
The first line must say what the video is about. Say it in the audio, show it in the visuals, write it in the text or caption - ideally all three. Priority number one is to set context immediately: even delaying it to the back half of the first sentence raises the skip rate sharply.

## 2. Lean
Get the viewer curious in a certain direction, so they lean in on the context and actively want to hear more. Ways to do it:
- proof or a stat that validates the incoming claim
- a specific pain point they have, or a benefit they want
- something interesting, shocking or unexpected
This opens the curiosity loop. The viewer must lean in instead of opting out.

## 3. Contrarian snapback
Say something that pulls the viewer away from the direction they were leaning, snapping them down another path. Like catching a fish: get the hook in its mouth swimming one way, then yank the line the other way. Context -> lean -> snapback is the full formula.

## Test
Read the first 1-2 sentences back on their own. Is the context immediately clear? Is there curiosity that makes the viewer lean in? Does one line then turn them another way?

## Output
Give the hook as three labelled lines (Context / Lean / Snapback) plus the on-screen text and first visual that carry the context. Do not give away the answer in the hook.
Studio use: applies to the teaser and to the first 10 s of every chapter (reel); see `guides/SCRIPT_STYLE.md`.
